# Tribe already fixes copy trading's costliest failure

The worst thing that happens to copy traders is a leader blowing up with nothing between that loss and the follower's account. Close behind are track records that cannot be trusted and fills that are structurally worse than the leader's. Tribe's attached stop-loss brackets, per-follower limits and the onchain CopyGuard halt already answer the first of these better than any exchange product in the evidence. Its Chainlink-attested leaderboard gives it the strongest base in the market for the second. The evidence for these three is strong: platforms admit them in their own help pages, Bitget admitted ROI manipulation in June 2026, and a WWW '26 study found that common wallet-copy strategies give copiers **negative average returns**. Tribe is weakest where users have lost the most money in single incidents, which is custody. Its server-held demo keys carry the same structural risk that cost 3Commas users a verified **$27.3M** and DEXX users about **$21M**. It also has no ledger or alerting yet for skipped or failed copies, the problem that makes users on ZuluTrade and similar platforms call copy trading "a scam". One part of the current design also works against Tribe. Creators are paid a share of fees on copy volume, which is the pay structure ESMA flags as a conflict of interest. The next builds with the biggest selling-point gain are, in order: non-custodial wallets with limits enforced when each transaction is signed; a per-trade copy ledger with real-time alerts; leaderboard metrics based on what copiers actually made; and creator pay linked to copier outcomes. One caution applies throughout: user-voice evidence comes almost entirely from Trustpilot, because Reddit and the app stores could not be sampled, and several popular statistics (for example "only 43.6% of lead traders made money for followers") have no traceable source.

## Thirteen pain points ranked by frequency times severity

Each pain point below is scored 1–5 on frequency (how many users meet it, across exchange copy trading, bot platforms, Telegram bots, onchain copy trading and signal groups) and 1–5 on severity (5 means money lost outright or the account wiped; 3 means returns eroded or serious friction; 1 means annoyance). The product of the two sets the rank. Ties are broken by how many users lose money and how strong the evidence is. The scores are judgement calls built from the evidence that follows, not measured rates. Rank 6 is a platform problem rather than a copy-trading problem; it is included because it is the largest complaint category in user reviews.

The custody rank rests on well-documented incidents with conflicting loss totals. The 3Commas figures range from "at least $6M" to $27.3M, and Banana Gun from $1.9M to $3M. The pattern is clear even though the exact figures are not. The user-voice frequencies come from about 165 unique Trustpilot reviews, coded by a summarising tool into multi-label categories. They are good for ranking only to about ±30%, and they over-represent angry users and money-access disputes. **Reddit, X, App Store and Google Play could not be sampled at all**. As a result, there is no direct evidence on panic-unfollowing, FOMO copying, or explicit requests for features like approve-before-copy, and Binance, OKX and Telegram-bot frequencies could not be grounded in review counts. Three widely repeated statistics should stay out of any pitch. The second is an unsourced "80% of copiers lose" headline. The third is a blog "audit" claiming providers earned 38.6% while followers realised 9.4%, which has only a thin method description ([influencerq](https://influencerq-web.pages.dev/research/how-slippage-and-fees-destroy-copy-trading-returns)). Most peer-reviewed evidence also comes from FX and stock social trading (eToro, wikifolio) or Solana memecoins, not from Bybit, Binance or Bitget copy data or from Base. Tribe's ETH-focused market sits between those settings.

## The top three failures all begin with the leader

### 1. A leader's blow-up reaches followers unfiltered (score 20, Tribe: Fixed)

The arc in user reviews is consistent: good-looking stats, a sudden wipe-out, then a conclusion that the whole platform is fake. One ZuluTrade reviewer wrote that "a week later the entire portfolio was wiped" on a $2,000 account. Another wrote that "the leaders may look profitable but eventually they all have lossess that are not managed at all" ([Trustpilot ZuluTrade](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com); [p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com?page=2)). An eToro user was "after like 12 month copying is -20%" ([eToro 1-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/etoro.com?stars=1)). Australia's ASIC investigated after a single rogue lead trader wiped out about **1,200 Australians' accounts** ([FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/regulatory/australias-asic-investigating-copy-trading-smh/)). The platforms themselves tolerate this. Binance's top lead-trader badges allow a **7-day maximum drawdown of 35%**, and its entry badge allows **50%** ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)). On futures, followers inherit the leader's leverage: a 10x position that drops 10% liquidates the copier ([Binance blog](https://www.binance.com/blog/futures/copy-trading-essential-criteria-for-selecting-lead-traders-5061215295703009149)). Onchain is no better. Cielo's official copy-trading risk page offers buy caps and market-cap filters but says nothing about stop-losses ([Cielo docs](https://docs.cielo.finance/guides/copy-trading/risks-and-considerations)). A Hyperliquid vault depositor's only control is to withdraw, after a lockup ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-depositors)). James Wynn showed how large the all-or-nothing outcome can be when **about $99.3M** of BTC longs were liquidated in one event ([Arkham](https://info.arkm.com/research/james-wynn-liquidated-twice)).

Research explains why leaders blow up. In a lab experiment, the option to copy amplified excessive risk-taking ([Apesteguia, Oechssler & Weidenholzer, Management Science 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:66:y:12:i:2020:p:5608-5622)). Signal providers far ahead of or behind their peers shift into lottery-like stocks, "exposing signal followers to a lottery-like return structure" ([Oehler & Schneider 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6)). Regulators have written the fix into supervisory expectations. ESMA asks whether firms have "(IT) arrangements in place to ensure that unsuitable transactions are not automatically copied", and whether they monitor copied trades against client risk limits and intervene ([ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)).

**Tribe status: Fixed, and this is its strongest claim.** Every entry can carry a take-profit and stop-loss bracket that is copied as one order and sized from the follower's actual fill. Followers can refuse any trade without a stop. Daily caps, per-trader budgets and a per-trade maximum bound the damage. CopyGuard halts a trader on drawdown, trade bursts or concentration, and Tribe reads that verdict from the contract immediately before every copy, so a halt holds even if an event is missed. This is close to a literal implementation of ESMA's "unsuitable transactions are not automatically copied". Two refinements would make the claim airtight. The first is a per-leader follower drawdown stop expressed in the follower's own money ("stop copying Alex if my copies of Alex are down $200"), if the pause line does not already do this. The second is showing Ask Cat's "worst case under your rules" in dollars on the copy button itself, not only when the user asks. If Tribe stays on spot orders, there is also no inherited liquidation price, which is worth saying explicitly against futures copy products.

### 2. Track records are gameable, and every platform's are self-reported (score 20, Tribe: Partly)

The clearest single piece of evidence is Bitget's own announcement of 12 June 2026. It said "a small number of elite traders were able to artificially inflate their ROI rankings" by opening a small position, depositing a large sum, trading and withdrawing. The old formula divided by "initial assets". The fix applies only to trades after the update ([Bitget announcement](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605456280)). Binance's rules show it watches for the same games: "historical closed portfolios", "fund transfer frequency" and carrying orders. The penalty is loss of the leader's benefits, not compensation for copiers ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)). Onchain, the tricks are cruder. One Telegram channel openly advises cropping GMGN screenshots to show only the ticker and "Total Profit" ([t.me/fastgemlowcap](https://t.me/s/fastgemlowcap/117)). Wash trading between self-owned wallets makes scanners flag a wallet as profitable ([Gemstone Labs](https://xxxdimacion.gitbook.io/gemstone-labs/ultimate-memecoin-guide/fake-profitable-wallets)). One unverified first-person account describes a wallet showing **+$247k realized at an 85% win rate** while sitting on **−$489k unrealized** ([teletype.in](https://teletype.in/@mr.ponder/i4kUlsmz9z0)). Brokers market survivorship directly, as in eToro's "If you invested $10K in these Popular Investors" post ([eToro blog](https://www.etoro.com/blog/from-etoro/if-you-invested-10k-in-these-popular-investors-heres-how-much-youd-have-today/)). A German consumer body sued NAGA over copy-trading marketing that stressed "alleged profits and high hit rates" while hiding risk. The suit was dismissed as inadmissible and is under appeal ([Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/verbandsklagen/klage-gegen-naga-markets-europe-ltd-99812)). Users read all of this as fraud: "there are no traders,the boards are fake". One provider reported "my account 80% profits and in my dashboard 0.15%" ([Trustpilot ZuluTrade](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com); [p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com?page=2)).

The academic evidence matters most here, because it shows that even honest leaderboards mislead when they rank the wrong thing. Investing in the traders with the highest accumulated returns "leads to high losses". No strategy earned a positive abnormal return after costs. A leader's follower count showed no positive relation to returns, while Sharpe-based selection did better ([Dorfleitner et al. 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v70y2018icp160-171.html)). Money flows chase raw returns rather than alpha, and are driven by how visible a portfolio is on the platform ([Röder & Walter 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v42y2019i2p383-411.html)).

**Tribe status: Partly, from the strongest starting position in the market.** A CRE workflow recomputes returns from Flash's order data rather than Tribe's database and attests the ranking hash onchain. That directly answers the "self-reported by the exchange" problem. Manual and Bot records are kept separate, which stops a bot's lucky run from inflating a person's record. What it does not yet answer is *what* is ranked. A "profit" board and a "most copied" board reproduce the exact flows Röder & Walter and Dorfleitner warn about. To close the gap, the attested computation should cover four more things. First, mark-to-market returns that include open losses. Second, returns adjusted for deposits and withdrawals, using a maximum-available-capital denominator like Bitget's fix. Third, a copier-outcome line ("what people who copied this trader actually made, net of fees"), which IOSCO asks intermediaries to review ([CMS on IOSCO](https://cms.law/en/int/regulatory-news/iosco-online-imitative-trading-practices-copy-trading-mirror-trading-social-trading)). Fourth, minimum trade counts and a concentration flag before anyone ranks. A record that starts only at registration also stops people from importing a cherry-picked winning wallet. The selling point becomes: "Our leaderboard is recomputed by Chainlink nodes from exchange fills, includes open losses, and shows what copiers made, not just what the leader made."

### 3. Followers fill worse than leaders by construction (score 20, Tribe: Partly)

Every venue admits this in its own documentation. Bybit fills follower entries and exits "using market orders, which are subject to slippage". It lists seven reasons a copy is skipped, and in Smart Copy mode it upsizes a small follower's order to the exchange minimum, so a small account commits a larger share of its balance than the leader did ([Bybit Help Center](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Understanding-Discrepancies-in-Copy-Trading-Positions-between-Master-Trader-s-and-Follower-s)). Bitget does not copy a leader's orders beyond **3 within one second**, caps quantity by order-book depth, and can leave hedged positions one-sided ([Bitget Support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603883269)). Binance caps copy slippage at 0.3–0.5% and warns that this can leave the follower with a smaller position than the lead trader ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/ro/support/faq/b9e5e3b2141149be826685d2c88536fa)). MQL5 has hosted the same complaint for about a decade. For signals with small take-profits, "slippage could negate almost all profit in your account" ([MQL5 forum #447735](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/447735)). On ZuluTrade, one user found "50 % of my real live account are not been copied" ([Trustpilot ZuluTrade](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com)).

Onchain, the gap becomes a loss. On 6,000 pump.fun projects, copiers "strictly overpay on every replicated buy". Wallet-selection based on simple statistics produced **negative average copier returns**, and even the best multi-agent system reached about **3% per coin against the smart-money wallets' 14%** ([Luo et al., arXiv 2601.08641, WWW '26](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641)). High slippage settings make copiers easy prey. One private mempool operator ran **1.55M sandwich attacks for about $13.43M** in a single month, and Helius notes that memecoin traders are "especially susceptible" because they set loose slippage ([Helius](https://helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)).

**Tribe status: Partly.** Protective orders are sized from the follower's actual fill, so the bracket never mismatches the position, which is a real improvement on percentage-mapped partial closes elsewhere. Every trade has a settlement receipt. Ask Cat already checks "whether you'd be chasing". Trading ETH-focused, deeper-liquidity markets on Base should shrink the imitation penalty compared with bonding curves. That is an inference, and Tribe should measure it before claiming it. Three pieces are missing. The first is a side-by-side record of the leader's fill and the follower's fill on every copy, aggregated into a "copy gap" figure on each leader profile, as MQL5's slippage tab does. The second is a chase limit for auto-copies that also covers exits: "skip if price has moved more than X% since the leader's fill", or copy as a Flash limit order at the leader's price. The third is confirmation of what MEV protection Flash routing gives on Base, so the claim can be made precisely. Tribe should also confirm that it never upsizes a copy beyond the follower's ratio, and that it skips and explains instead. The selling point: "Tribe would rather skip a trade than overpay for it, and it shows your fill next to the leader's every time."

## Custody, insiders and support are where Tribe is weakest

### 4. Custody is the single largest source of incident losses (score 15, Tribe: Not yet)

The largest bot-platform losses came from keys, not strategies. HAPI verified **$27,285,845 in losses across 86 3Commas users in 32 countries**. The attackers used leaked trade-only keys to buy illiquid tokens from their own sell orders, turning one victim's 50 BTC into 7 BTC without any withdrawal permission ([ForkLog / HAPI](https://forklog.com/en/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident/)). 3Commas called the reports "false rumors" before confirming the leak. Its CEO argued that paying victims would mean "paying for users to lose their API keys" ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/crypto-platform-3commas-admits-hackers-stole-api-keys)). Custodial Telegram-style bots fail the same way at smaller scale but more often. DEXX lost about **$21M** through "improper management of private keys" ([Brave New Coin / SlowMist](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/dexx-hack-investigation-unveils-over-8600-solana-wallet-links-slowmist-report)). Banana Gun lost about **$3M** from 11 users ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/318074/banana-gun-exploit-refund)). Maestro lost about 280 ETH to a router bug ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/maestro-trading-bot-refunds-610-eth-to-users-following-router-exploit/)), Unibot about $640K ([Rekt](https://alpha.rekt.news/unibot-rekt)), and Solareum about $523K from 300+ users before it shut down ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/224371/solana-telegram-trading-bot-shut-down-users-drained-523k)). Maestro, Trojan and Banana Gun all generate and store user keys on their servers ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/maestro-vs-trojan-vs-banana-gun)). Telegram itself is an attack surface. Scam Sniffer reported a **2000% rise** in malicious Telegram groups that use fake "verification bots" ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/malicious-telegram-group-scams-have-increased-2000-since-november-scam-sniffer)). How users reacted depended on compensation. Banana Gun refunded from its treasury and its token rose about 7% ([CoinGape](https://coingape.com/banana-price-jumps-7-as-banana-gun-issues-full-refund-to-hack-victims)). 3Commas' denial drew an FBI probe ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/fbi-probes-3commas-api-key-leak-reports-say/)).

**Tribe status: Not yet, and this is the biggest gap in the pitch.** Server-held demo keys put Tribe in the same structural category as the custodial bots above. Ask-first approvals over Telegram also create a phishing surface that cloned bots will copy. The planned Privy embedded wallets are the fix, but only if they are paired with signing-time policy. Infrastructure vendors describe policy engines that enforce allowlists, spending limits and calldata limits at signing time "rather than in bot logic", which a compromised bot could bypass ([Turnkey](https://www.turnkey.com/blog/designing-telegram-trading-bots-patterns-primitives-and-security)). For Tribe, that means a session signer limited to Flash order contracts, bounded by the follower's own max-per-trade and daily cap, with no transfer rights. Withdrawals would stay with the user's own key. Tribe should publish that policy and the official Telegram bot handle. The selling point, true only once built: "Tribe can place your copy trades, but it cannot move your money. Your limits are enforced by the wallet itself, not just by our server."

### 5. Insider wallets, paid shills and pump groups pose as smart money (score 15, Tribe: Partly)

In memecoin and signal-group copying, the person being copied is often the person selling to you. ZachXBT found that more than 100 crypto influencers took undisclosed promo deals. In a leaked sheet of **160+ influencers, fewer than 5% labelled ads**, at **$50 to $60,000 per post** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/368956/zachxbt-says-over-100-crypto-influencers-accepted-promo-deals-without-disclosing-paid-ads)). One operator ran **16 X accounts** that looked like independent consensus ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/zachxbt-exposes-16-x-meme-coin-accounts)). On $LIBRA, **82% of supply sat in one wallet cluster** and about **$87M was drained in roughly an hour**, with investor losses above $250M ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/the-libra-playbook-how-one-cluster-drained-87-million-in-a-single-hour/)). HAWK launched with 96% of supply in one cluster ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/hawk-anatomy-of-a-celebrity-rug/)). Bundle bots appear in about a quarter of pump.fun projects ([arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641)). Pump groups are long-standing and large: **2,469 Telegram and 952 Discord pumps** in six months of 2018 ([HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/an-examination-of-the-cryptocurrency-pump-and-dump-ecosystem/)), with late participants taking the losses ([arXiv 2306.02148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.02148v1)). The SEC charged eight influencers over an alleged ~$100M scheme run partly through a 233,000-member Discord ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/14/sec-charges-social-media-influencers-in-alleged-100-million-fraud-scheme.html)). Even the trackers are conflicted: Kolscan was reportedly acquired by Pump.fun, whose tokens it ranks ([Kalkine](https://kalkinemedia.com/education/guides/kolscan-how-solanas-smart-money-gets-ranked)).

Frequency is scored at 3 rather than 5 only because the problem concentrates in memecoin and signal-group segments, not exchange copy trading. Inside those segments it is close to universal.

**Tribe status: Partly.** CopyGuard checks flagged tokens, concentration and trade bursts, and compares the Chainlink ETH/USD feed with the DEX price, which catches thin or manipulated pricing. ETH focus keeps most users away from the bonding-curve fights. Tribe's structural advantage is that feed cards are built from settled fills, so a call cannot be a cropped screenshot. What is missing is the signal that matters most: whether the caller is exiting into the people copying them. Tribe should show post-to-position timing on every card ("bought 2h before posting, still holding" or "sold 10 minutes after posting"). It should alert followers when a leader sells while copies are still being opened, and keep a running "sold into copiers" count on profiles. It should require a sponsored tag on paid posts, enforced by withholding creator fee share on undisclosed promotions. Before Tribe expands beyond ETH, it should add holder-cluster and deployer-overlap checks. The selling point: "On Tribe, a call is a trade with a receipt. You can see when the caller bought, whether they still hold, and whether they are selling while you are buying."

### 6. Support failures and frozen withdrawals dominate reviews (score 15, Tribe: Not yet)

This is the most common complaint in the sample. Support failures account for about **64 tagged mentions** and frozen withdrawals or locked accounts for about **40**, across about 143 complaining Trustpilot reviews. One Bybit user had about $30K frozen with "no way to withdraw my own funds" ([Trustpilot Bybit](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.bybit.com)). An eToro user "withdrew money & havent received it" and reached only bots ([eToro 1-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/etoro.com?stars=1)). TrustScores reflect it: ZuluTrade 1.3, Bitget 1.7, Bybit 2.4, Pionex 2.5 ([Trustpilot ZuluTrade](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com); [Bitget](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bitget.com); [Pionex](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pionex.com)). This is a platform problem more than a copy-trading one: **none of 43 sampled Bybit and Bitget reviews mentioned copy trading**. Copy-specific pain on exchanges probably lives on Reddit and X, which could not be checked. It still matters to Tribe for two reasons. Unexplained execution failures get relabelled as "scam" when support does not answer. And an AI support bot is itself a complaint: "the ai chatbot in the app was useless" ([eToro 1-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/etoro.com?stars=1)).

**Tribe status: Not yet.** With server-held keys, Tribe is functionally a custodian, so a freeze is possible in principle. The non-custodial build from rank 4 removes the whole category. Onchain receipts already make every dispute checkable. Ask Cat can serve as a first-line explainer only if it answers with the specific receipts ("this copy was skipped because your daily cap was hit at 14:02; here is the tx"), not generic text, and hands off to a human. The selling point after the custody build: "Your funds sit in your own wallet onchain. There is no withdrawal queue to get stuck in."

## Smaller frictions decide whether users trust the next copy

### 7. Copying breaks silently (score 12, Tribe: Partly)

On the dedicated copy platform, the most common practical failure is that the copy link breaks and nobody tells the user. ZuluTrade reviews include "Copy trading disconnect and you know nothing", "Positions remain open indefinitely on followers' accounts", "the app won't let me stop copying" (about $688 lost), an outage that "couldn't restore the connection for a week and I lost 700 euros", trades copied at 10:1 instead of 1:1, and "my account started opening trades without my permission" ([Trustpilot ZuluTrade](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com); [p2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/zulutrade.com?page=2)). cTrader has a thread titled "Trade closed in Strategy Provider account but not in 2 Copying accounts" ([cTrader forum](https://community.ctrader.com/forum/ctrader-copy/23666)). On MEXC, a follower who keeps "Do Not Follow" switched on keeps an open position after the leader is liquidated ([MEXC learn](https://www.mexc.com/learn/article/17827791509171)). A Cryptohopper user found that TradingView webhooks fired no trades in seven months ([Trustpilot Cryptohopper](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/cryptohopper.com)).

**Tribe status: Partly.** Tribe's dead-man's switch is rare in this market and worth leading with. If CRE goes silent or the chain cannot be read, auto-copies fail safe into ask-first instead of failing open. The guard log and receipts give a full audit trail. The known limitation, "alerts for some events not yet sent", is exactly the gap users complain about. The fix is a copy ledger: for every leader trade, show whether it was copied, skipped, declined or halted, with the reason (cap, budget, no stop, guard halt, chase limit) and both fills. Every skip, halt and fallback should trigger a push or Telegram alert within seconds. A visible heartbeat ("CopyGuard last checked this trader 40s ago") completes it. The brief does not say whether a leader's manual exit, as opposed to a bracket exit, is mirrored to followers. That should be confirmed and shown in the ledger, because orphaned positions are a documented failure. The selling point: "Tribe never fails silently. Every skipped or halted copy tells you why within seconds, and if our safety checks go quiet, Tribe stops copying on its own and asks you first."

### 8. Loss of control over each trade (score 12, Tribe: Fixed)

Users cannot choose the risk, stop or size per trade. That is the core grievance in a widely shared post titled "Copy Trading Made Me 60% Profit Then Destroyed Everything" ([TradingView](https://es.tradingview.com/chart/BTCUSDT/VH2GG0Zd-Copy-Trading-Made-Me-60-Profit-Then-Destroyed-Everything)). eToro users in October 2026 wrote "Can't set stoploss" and "I CANT EDIT A STOP LOSS!!!" after an app update ([eToro 1-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/etoro.com?stars=1)). Forced platform changes cause the same loss of control. 3Commas' V2 migration left one user with "none of my 100+ bots were preserved", with DCA bots shut down at a loss ([Trustpilot 3Commas](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/3commas.io)).

**Tribe status: Fixed.** Ask-first with a timer (in-app or Telegram), copy ratio, max per trade, daily cap, per-trader budget, stop-only copying, pause-all, and gasless signed updates to move a live stop together cover every complaint above. There is also a regulatory reason to make ask-first prominent. ESMA treats services that "automatically execute" signals as portfolio management, and says clicking "autocopy" effectively gives the firm a mandate. Services where the client acts before execution may instead be advice or reception and transmission of orders ([ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)). An ask-first default is both the safer product and the lighter regulatory posture. One lesson from 3Commas: promise in writing that rules and bot configurations survive upgrades. The selling point: "Approve each copy in one tap from Telegram, or let it run inside limits you set, and move a live stop in a second without paying gas."

### 9. Leader pay rewards volume and gambling (score 12, Tribe: Partly, and a liability)

Binance pays lead traders "a 10% profit share from their copy traders; and a 10% commission from their copy traders' trading fee" ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)). The second stream pays leaders for copier turnover whether copiers win or lose. ESMA tells supervisors to examine variable pay based on the "number and value of trades copied", the number of followers or AUM, and treats such commissions as possible inducements that must be disclosed to copiers ([ESMA35-42-1428, section 2.6](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)). The behavioural literature adds that becoming a copied leader *increases* the disposition effect, holding losers to avoid admitting mistakes ([Pelster & Hofmann 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v94y2018icp75-88.html)), and that tournament incentives push underperformers to take more risk ([Oehler & Schneider 2023, citing Kirchler et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6)).

**Tribe status: Partly, and the current design is a liability.** A share of the Flash integrator fee on copies is pay per value of trades copied, the structure ESMA singles out. It is not a profit share, so it does not reward gambling directly, but it does reward churn. CopyGuard's trade-burst check is a partial counterweight. To turn this into a selling point, Tribe should do three things. Disclose on every copy what the creator earns from it. Hold each season's creator share and release it in proportion to copiers' net outcome, with nothing earned on copies made while a trader was halted or in a grey zone. Fund the "safest" board's rewards from copier results rather than leader profit. The selling point: "Creators on Tribe earn more when their copiers make money, and you can see exactly what they earn from your copy."

### 10. Hidden fees and small-balance traps (score 12, Tribe: Partly)

Hidden fees account for about 14 mentions and billing disputes for about 19 in the review sample. 3Commas buries "the real cost in tiny font at the bottom of the payment page" ([Trustpilot 3Commas](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/3commas.io)). One eToro user who stopped copying was "charged a flat $1.00 - $2.00 commission on EVERY SINGLE micro-ticket" left over from the copy ([eToro 1-star](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/etoro.com?stars=1)). Pionex asked for a 20 USDT recovery fee to return $18.90 ([Trustpilot Pionex](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/pionex.com)). Telegram bots charge about **0.5–1% per trade** before priority fees and MEV ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/maestro-vs-trojan-vs-banana-gun)). ESMA expects costs disclosed up front and gross performance figures to show the effect of fees ([ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)).

**Tribe status: Partly.** There is no subscription, and cancels and updates are gasless. Two things are missing. The first is an all-in cost preview on each copy, in dollars and as a percentage of the position (Flash fee, integrator fee, creator share, expected slippage), with a warning when a small ratio makes fees a large share of the trade. The second is a one-tap "close everything from this leader" action after unfollowing, so no dust positions are left behind. The selling point: "Before you copy, Tribe shows the full cost in dollars, and warns you when fees would eat the trade."

## Hidden harms that users rarely name but regulators and researchers do

### 11. Bot opacity, regime risk and fake bots (score 9, Tribe: Partly)

Grid and DCA bots leave users holding losing positions when price trends out of range ([Gainium](https://gainium.io/best/grid)). Cryptohopper reviews note backtests that do not survive live trading and strategies that "struggle in downtrends" ([Bitget Academy](https://www.bitget.cloud/academy/cryptohopper-review)). One user bought a marketplace strategy that lost money for six months and concluded "the whole platform is a scam" ([Trustpilot Cryptohopper](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/cryptohopper.com)). At the extreme, Mirror Trading International took **29,421 BTC (>$1.7B)** with a trading bot that, according to the CFTC, did not exist ([CoinDesk](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/policy/2022/06/30/cftc-charges-south-african-bitcoin-club-mti-in-17b-fraud)). The CFTC now treats promised returns from "AI bots" as a red flag ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)). The SEC fined Delphia $225,000 and Global Predictions $175,000 in its first "AI-washing" cases ([O'Melveny](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/sec-targets-ai-washing-in-charges-against-two-investment-advisers)).

**Tribe status: Partly.** Separate Manual and Bot records, user-owned bots that trade under the owner's name, and onchain receipts for every bot fill are the opposite of the MTI template. Scoring Ask Cat's own calls in public is the best defence against AI-washing, and Tribe should say so. One feature needs care. ESMA bars simulated past performance from being used to suggest future performance, and wants real past performance shown over at least a full 12 months, ideally five years ([ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)). The 30-day "what your rules would have done" simulation is useful, but it should be labelled as hypothetical, show the worst day alongside the total, and never appear in marketing as an expected return. The selling point: "Every bot on Tribe trades in public under its owner's name, with a separate record and a receipt for every fill."

### 12. Gamification and herding push users toward risk (score 9, Tribe: Partly)

Users do not complain about this, but the research is consistent. Hedonic gamification raised trading volume by about 5%, and lower-literacy investors self-select into gamified apps ([Chapkovski, Khapko & Zoican, Management Science 2024](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/epdf/10.1287/mnsc.2022.02650)). In a 605-person study, badges and nudges significantly increased risk-taking, and a one-standard-deviation rise in financial literacy cut that effect by 56% ([JBEF 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/beexfi/v46y2025ics2214635025000309.html)). Seeing better-performing peers increased risk-taking among 807 retail investors ([Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49648-3.pdf)). The stocks Robinhood users herded into most showed about **−4.7% abnormal returns over 20 days** ([Barber et al., Journal of Finance 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:6:p:3141-3190)). ASIC's case against eToro alleges a screening test that was "very difficult to fail" and prompted users toward passing answers ([ASIC 23-204MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2023-releases/23-204mr-asic-sues-etoro-in-its-first-design-and-distribution-action-to-protect-consumers-from-high-risk-cfd-products/)).

**Tribe status: Partly.** Rewarding brackets, stop-protected copies and research before copying inverts the harmful pattern, and that is a real differentiator. The remaining risk lies in streaks, "most copied" and profit boards, reactions, and the story rail, which together form an attention engine. Streaks should count only safe behaviour, never trade count or winning streaks. "Safest" should be the default leaderboard tab. A crowding notice should appear when many followers pile into one trade at once ("900 copies in the last hour; late copies fill worse"). The selling point: "Tribe's badges reward stops and research, never trading more."

### 13. Transparency becomes an attack surface (score 8, Tribe: Partly)

Hyperliquid shows the downside of full transparency. Its protocol vault absorbed at least four manipulation or liquidation events in 2025. JELLY left an unrealized loss reported at $10–13.5M before validators delisted the token ([CoinDesk](https://hotfix.coindesk.com/markets/2025/03/26/hyperliquid-delists-jellyjelly-after-vault-squeezed-in-usd13m-tussle)). POPCAT cost about $5M and paused deposits and withdrawals ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/378606/hyperliquid-pauses-deposits-withdrawals-popcat-trading-scheme-speculation)). Wynn's published liquidation levels were broadcast in real time while he was liquidated ([Arkham](https://info.arkm.com/research/james-wynn-liquidated-twice)). 10x Research calls this a crowd-led "people's hunt" ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/news/hyperliquid-opens-the-door-to-a-crowd-led-whale-hunt-says-10x-research)). Vault depositors also face 1-day (user vault) and 4-day (protocol vault) lockups ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-depositors)).

**Tribe status: Partly.** There is no pooled vault and no lockup, so the run-on-the-vault dynamic does not apply. There is a new risk that Tribe's own design creates. Because brackets are copied together, every follower's stop sits at the same price, and public portfolio tabs show stop and target levels. On a thin token, that is a visible liquidity cliff that someone could push price into. Tribe could show stops as "protected, about 4% below" rather than an exact price, offer an optional small stop offset per follower, and cap copy size relative to pool liquidity. The selling point: "No lockups and no pooled vault: your copies are yours to stop at any moment."

## Conclusion

The evidence changes where the competition lies. Exchanges have spent years adding refusal-to-copy caps, slippage guards and tier systems. Each trades one failure for another, such as skipped trades or one-sided books, and none compensates followers or shows what copiers actually earned. The academic record says the deeper problem is selection: ranking by raw return attracts money and predicts nothing. So the copy trading that wins is not the one that copies fastest. It filters leaders on behaviour, makes each copy's risk explicit before it happens, and proves outcomes afterwards. Tribe's CopyGuard plus its attested leaderboard is already that design, and the WWW '26 result that better wallet selection matters more than execution speed supports it.

The strategic risk is that Tribe's best features rest on two foundations that are not finished. One is custody: a server holding keys. The other is pay: fees earned on copy volume. Both are exactly what the incident and regulatory record punishes. Fixing them does more than remove liabilities. It lets Tribe make claims no exchange or Telegram bot can make at the same time: the platform cannot take your money, cannot fake its rankings, and does not profit when you lose. Tribe should also commission its own measurement, from Flash data on Base, of copier versus leader returns and of how often stops actually fired. Today the market's most-quoted copier statistics are unverifiable, and the first platform to publish a real one, net of fees and attested onchain, will own that claim.
