# Sell creator bots as guardrails, not returns

Tribe should launch Creator Studio as **"strategies with receipts"**: a place where Base traders publish manual trades and simple rule-based bots that run in their own policy-limited wallets, under CopyGuard, with every fill attested onchain. It should not be sold as "AI that trades for you" or as "copy the pros and profit". Competitors make publishing almost free and make earning hard. Bitget lets any user mark a new bot copyable in one click, then gates higher payouts behind tiers tied to assets copied, copier PnL and drawdown. Tribe should copy that shape: anyone can publish, and payouts and ranking unlock only with a verified record. On money, the market offers three models. Exchanges charge a performance fee of **10% by default, up to 30%**. eToro pays a platform-funded **1.5%/yr on assets copied**. Cryptohopper sells strategies and keeps **30%** of each sale. The 2026 winners on Base and Solana (Fomo, Hyperliquid builders, Telegram bots) take money per trade instead, at **0.05% to 1%**. Tribe's Flash-fee share fits that per-trade model and should stay, with no subscription and no profit share at launch. Paid purely on volume, though, it is the structure ESMA flags as a conflict and it rewards churn. So part of each creator's share should be held back each season and released according to what copiers actually made. Regulation pushes the same way. Auto-executing a creator's bot in followers' wallets looks like portfolio management in the EU, a commodity trading adviser (CTA) or investment adviser in the US, and a regulated promotion in the UK. User-confirmed copies, identical non-personalised strategy information, geofencing and creator KYC before payouts are therefore the defensible default. The MVP is a real trade ticket (with Ask Cat as a drafting aid, not the only way in), a bot builder that forks the existing CRE strategy into three parameterised templates, a publish wizard that actually starts a bot in the creator's own Privy policy wallet, and a copy ledger. The launch is cheap: a founding creator cohort of 20–30, a 4–6 week Season 1 with a small prize pool split 50/50 between creators and copiers and ranked on copier outcomes, a 25% declining referral share, shareable receipt cards, and Base grants. One caution: almost no platform publishes creator earnings, copier retention or CAC. The pricing below is a reasoned recommendation, not a benchmark-proven optimum.

## Competitors make publishing free and earning hard

Across exchanges the pattern is consistent. It costs almost nothing to become copyable and a lot to earn well. Bitget's manual "Elite Trader" route asks for at least **100 USDT**, one closed trade and one enabled copy pair, bans following other elites, and allows one elite account per person ([Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603777069)). Its bot route is even lighter. Anyone can apply from the bot homepage or accept a one-click upgrade popup when creating a first bot. The creator sets the pair, investment, grid settings and a profit-share preset, and the bot becomes copyable immediately. All copy bots are public, and bots built from Bitget's own AI-recommended parameters are excluded from the elite list ([Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603838661)). Bitget extended bot copy trading in January 2026 ([Bitget blog](https://www.bitget.cloud/blog/articles/bitget-bot-copy-trading-launch)). OKX requires full KYC for spot lead traders, and new leaders start at level 1 ([OKX FAQ](https://www.okx.com/en-eu/help/lead-traders-faq)). Binance allows one lead portfolio per user and gates its upgraded Lead Trader Growth Plan on margin balance, 30-day copier PnL, 7-day max drawdown and assets copied, reviewed at its discretion ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/3160c961b2734d1cbb5342d1e86c6cdb)). The outlier is eToro, which is curated. It requires **$2,000 equity and two months of history** to enter, enforces a risk score of at most 7, weekly drawdown no worse than −25%, no single position above 50%, and at least one 100-word post a month, and pays nothing until a trader has **five verified copiers** ([eToro Popular Investor](https://www.etoro.com/copytrader/popular-investor/)).

Onchain products swap editorial gates for capital gates. A Hyperliquid vault leader seeds **100 USDC**, must hold **at least 5%** of the vault, cannot withdraw below that, and earns **10%** of profits ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders)). Drift vaults are permissionless, but fees are fixed at creation and can only go down, charged per depositor above a high-water mark ([Drift docs](https://docs.drift.trade/developers/vault-managers)). The consumer winners skip creator packaging entirely. Fomo reached **625K+ users and $4B+ volume** by June 2026 and raised at a **$550M valuation** with feeds, leaderboards and copy trading, but no profit share for the traders being copied was found ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/fomo-raise-series-b-trading-app-550m-valuation); [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-app-explained)). Base itself turned "trading-first, with social layers (copy-trading, feed-trading, leaderboards) on top" in January 2026, and ended Creator Rewards a month later ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/base-app-trading-strategy-pivot); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/base-app-shifts-to-trading-first-strategy-maintains-mini-apps-and-creator-coins)).

| Product | What a creator publishes | Barrier to publish | How creators are paid | Lesson for Tribe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bitget Elite / Bot copy | Manual trades; grid and futures-grid bots | ≥100 USDT, one account, one click for bots | 10–20% profit share by tier; bots 0/10/20/30% presets | One-click "make copyable" at bot creation |
| Binance lead trader | One lead portfolio | Low; tiers by PnL, MDD, AUM | 10% profit share + 10% of copier fees; up to 30% elite | Fee commission pays even when copiers lose |
| OKX lead trader | Spot/futures trades | KYC | Up to 30%; spot 8–13% by AUM; weekly netted | KYC the paid party |

Sources for the table: [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603777069), [Bitget bots](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603838661), [The Block on Binance](https://www.theblock.co/post/255396/binance-introduces-copy-trading-feature-for-futures-markets), [OKX profit sharing](https://www.okx.com/help/lead-traders-trader-profit-sharing-rules), [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/copytrader/popular-investor/), [Hyperliquid](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders), [Cryptohopper](https://docs.cryptohopper.com/docs/sellers/exclusivity-program), [Odaily on Fomo](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5212823).

Two packaging points matter most for Tribe. First, every serious platform treats **bot copy as a separate product from trade copy**. On Bitget the copier clones a running bot, with its parameters and its own lifecycle, and the copy ends when the creator stops the bot. Trade copy, by contrast, offers rich per-order controls ([Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603838661); [Bybit help](https://bybit.nl/en/help-center/article/Copy-Trading-Copy-Mode-and-Parameters-Settings)). Tribe does not need two copy engines. If a creator's bot trades in the creator's own wallet, followers copy its trades through the same pipeline that already attaches a stop to every copy. Bots then differ from people only in their label, their separate record and their disclosed rules. Second, the products that monetise the creator rather than the trade have faded. friend.tech's daily fees fell about **90%** within weeks of their August 2023 peak ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/friend-tech-daily-fees-buyers-drop-over-90/amp/)). Base Creator Rewards averaged about **$26 per creator** before closing ([eGamers](https://egamers.io/?p=105823)). Virtuals reported about $479M in "agent GDP" against only about **$452K** in actual ACP protocol revenue ([Fundstrat](https://fundstrat.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/20260624-Digital-Asset-Strategy-Virtuals-Protocol-–-Growing-Agentic-GDP-Q2-Update.pdf)). Creator Studio should be a way to make trades worth copying, not a creator token or a marketplace of paid strategies.

## Per-trade fees win, but pure volume pay is a liability

The fee data splits cleanly by model. Exchange profit shares cluster at **10% by default, rising to 13–30% with tier**. They are charged only on realised profit and netted against losses, and settled weekly on Binance and OKX or daily on Bitget ([OKX](https://www.okx.com/help/lead-traders-trader-profit-sharing-rules); [Binance](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/abc27f5949cf46d9801d31b5e33d0b38); [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603777069)). Platforms take very different cuts of that share. Pionex keeps **50%** of creator-set profit share on copies made through its Discover feed ([Pionex notice](https://intercom.help/pionex/en/articles/14613057-notice-regarding-the-adjustment-of-copy-trade-s-profit-sharing-fees)), and dHEDGE's DAO takes **10%** of manager fees ([dHEDGE docs](https://docs.dhedge.org/dhedge-protocol/dao-protocol-fees)). eToro instead pays from its own revenue: **1.5% a year on average assets copied**, with Cadet payouts floored at $250 and capped at $500 a month, while copiers pay no per-leader fee ([eToro](https://www.etoro.com/copytrader/popular-investor/)). Per-trade pricing sits at two levels. Memecoin bots charge about **1%** (GMGN, Photon, Trojan) or **0.5%** on Ethereum (Banana Gun) ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained); [BreakingAC](https://breakingac.com/news/2026/apr/30/multi-chain-telegram-trading-bots-head-to-head-trojan-vs-banana-gun-vs-maestro/)). Majors-focused front-ends charge far less: Phantom earned **$20.6M at a 0.05% builder fee**, MetaMask charges 0.1%, and Fomo charges about 0.5% spot and 0.05% on select majors ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/top-hyperliquid-builders); [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-app-explained)). Referral shares, the cheapest growth engine, run at **25%** (Fomo, whose referral payouts passed $2M) and follow declining schedules such as BONKbot's **30/20/10%** ([Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5212823); [Medium survey](https://medium.com/@0xkryptokeisarii/all-telegram-trading-bot-referral-systems-listed-april-2024-cd043095e2b4)).

Tribe's current "creators earn a share of Flash fees" model is closest to Binance's second stream, a **10% commission on copiers' trading fees** paid whether copiers win or lose ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/255396/binance-introduces-copy-trading-feature-for-futures-markets)). It has real strengths. It needs no high-water-mark accounting. Copiers pay no extra fee. And creators earn from day one, which makes recruiting them easier. It also has two weaknesses. ESMA tells supervisors to scrutinise variable pay based on the "number and value of trades copied", followers or assets copied, and to treat it as remuneration or an inducement that must be disclosed ([PwC Legal on ESMA35-42-1428](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services)). And it rewards turnover. Academic work finds signal providers face convex incentives and take more risk near their high-water mark ([Review of Managerial Science 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6)), and that copy trading itself raises risk-taking ([CEPR VoxEU](https://cepr.org/article/copy-trading-platforms-and-risk-taking)).

The recommended pricing keeps the per-trade model and changes when creators get paid. Copiers pay one all-in Flash/Tribe fee per copy, shown in dollars before they confirm, with no subscription and no profit share. Tribe's take should sit nearer the majors band (0.05–0.5%) than the 1% memecoin band, because Tribe trades deep markets and sells safety, not speed. The creator's cut of Tribe's fee on copied orders should be about **30% at launch, rising to 40–50%** for creators who clear quality tiers. That mirrors the industry pattern of a low default and a higher ceiling earned on a verified record. Half of each creator's share would be paid as earned, and the other half held for the season and released in proportion to copiers' net outcome on that creator's copies. Nothing would be earned on copies made while a leader was paused by CopyGuard. A separate **25% referral share**, declining over time, would go to whoever brought in the copier. A creator who brings their own audience would collect both streams, which is how the Telegram bots turned referrers into salespeople. A true performance fee (a 10% cap with a per-copier high-water mark, enforced in the attested CRE computation) belongs in a later version, once counsel has reviewed the profit-linked pay.

The economics need honest framing. Tribe's actual Flash fee rate was not in the research, so take an assumed **0.2%** Tribe fee as an illustration. A creator with 100 active copiers, each copying 20 trades a month at $100, generates $200K in copied volume. That is $400 in fees and about **$120 a month** for the creator at a 30% share. Cash alone will not recruit serious traders at the start. That is why the evidence-backed cold-start tools (status, seasons, prize pools, exposure) matter as much as the split, and why per-creator revenue should be reported as realised payouts, not notional volume.

## Trust comes from bounded bots and outcome-ranked records

The incident record says the attack surface is keys and backends, not strategies. Bankr disabled swaps after **14 wallets** were drained in May 2026, in what was attributed to prompt-injection-style social engineering or a custody-layer failure. Its wallets were auto-provisioned per X handle through a third-party custodian ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bankr-disables-transactions-after-14-wallets-hacked)). AIXBT's dashboard was used to queue prompts that sent about **55 ETH** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/310510/aixbt-ai-influencer-hacked-100k-ethereum)), and Banana Gun lost about **$3M** across 11 users through its Telegram backend ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/318074/banana-gun-exploit-refund)). Infrastructure vendors converged on policy engines that enforce contract allowlists, spending limits and calldata limits at signing time, "rather than in bot logic", which a compromised bot could bypass ([Turnkey](https://www.turnkey.com/blog/designing-telegram-trading-bots-patterns-primitives-and-security)). For Creator Studio, a published bot should therefore run only in the creator's **Privy policy-limited wallet**. That wallet's signer can call Flash order routes for allowlisted assets within per-trade and per-day caps, and cannot transfer funds. Followers keep their current split: MetaMask users approve every copy, and Privy users may opt into auto-copy within limits enforced by their own wallet policy. Ask Cat, as an LLM surface that can create trades, must sit under the same policy and confirmation step as any other trade, because prompt injection is now a documented loss vector and not a theory.

The second trust problem is whose record a follower is looking at. Onchain adversaries groom attractive wallets, accumulate in unlinked side wallets, and buy from the main wallet so copiers follow before the exit ([Arkham research](https://info.arkm.com/research/crypto-copy-trading-what-is-it-and-how-to-do-it); [Cielo docs](https://docs.cielo.finance/guides/copy-trading/risks-and-considerations)). On 6,000 pump.fun projects, copiers using simple wallet selection earned **negative average returns** ([Luo et al., WWW '26](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641)). Even honest leaderboards mislead when they rank raw return. Copying the highest accumulated returns "leads to high losses", and follower count showed no positive relation to returns ([Dorfleitner et al. 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v70y2018icp160-171.html)). Darwinex's answer is to normalise every strategy to the same risk target, a maximum of **6.5% monthly VaR**, and score it on attributes such as risk stability ([Darwinex](https://help.darwinex.com/risk-manager)). Bitget shows leaders their copier retention and time-weighted return ([Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603844617)). Tribe's existing eligibility checks (14+ days, 10+ trades, no Guard pauses in 30 days, stops on half of trades) are already stricter than any exchange's entry gate. The gap is what the profile shows once a creator passes. Ranking and payouts should use copier net outcome, mark-to-market drawdown and a "copy gap" between leader and copier fills. That moves CopyGuard from a pause button to the thing that decides who gets paid.

The bot-specific controls follow from this. Bot records must stay separate from the creator's manual record, as Tribe already does, so a lucky bot run cannot inflate a person's reputation. A new bot should run for at least **14 days with real but small capital** before it can be published, and the creator should keep a minimum stake in it. Hyperliquid's 100 USDC seed and 5% floor are the precedent ([Hyperliquid docs](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders)), and something like **$100 and at least 5% of the bot's average copied exposure** is a cheap, credible equivalent. Bots can trade at most 10 times a day (the existing /bot ceiling), only in allowlisted liquid assets, and every bot order carries a stop. Parameter changes take effect after a short notice period that is visible to followers. Bybit's "Forced Sync" shows the creator-side alternative: lock followers to a faithful replica in exchange for disclosure ([Bybit help](https://bybit.nl/en/help-center/article/Copy-Trading-Copy-Mode-and-Parameters-Settings)). Tribe should not offer that, because a copy stop chosen by the follower is the core promise. Anti-wash rules should withhold fee share on trades between linked wallets and on copies a creator's own cluster generates, which is the friend.tech lesson about fake accounts ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/crypto/2023/08/28/crypto-social-app-friend-tech-falls-flat)). Disclosure should be dynamic and backed by data. eToro refreshes its retail-loss percentage periodically and gives affiliates a tool to keep it current ([eToro Partners PDF](https://etoropartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Dynamic-disclaimer-percent-tool.pdf)). Tribe can go further and show, attested onchain, the share of copiers who made money on each creator, net of fees.

## Auto-copy and paid creators are the regulatory pressure points

Three design choices decide most of the regulatory exposure: whether copies execute automatically, how creators are paid, and where Tribe markets. In the EU, ESMA's 2023 briefing classifies copy trading case by case as portfolio management, investment advice, or reception and transmission of orders. Its MiCA Q&A 2463 applies that same logic to crypto-asset service providers ([ESMA briefing](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf); [MiCA Q&A 2463 mirror](https://micawtf.pages.dev/eu-level/q-and-a/esma-qa-2463-auto-trading/)). A follower who switches on auto-copy of a creator's bot is close to granting a portfolio-management mandate, which brings MiCA Article 81 suitability duties ([ESMA Single Rulebook](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-81-providing-advice-crypto-assets)). The briefing also expects copied traders to be selected and reviewed, past performance to be presented fairly, and pay based on trades copied, followers or assets copied to be managed as a conflict ([PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services)). In the UK, crypto promotions to retail have required a **24-hour cooling-off period**, personalised risk warnings and appropriateness checks since 8 October 2023. FCA guidance treats any commercial interest, including referral commission, as enough to make an influencer's post a financial promotion ([FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/fg23-3-finalised-non-handbook-guidance-cryptoasset-financial-promotions); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uk-fca-finalises-social-media-financial-promotions-guidance)). In the US, the CFTC publisher exclusion is lost once a provider directs client accounts or tailors advice ([CFTC Federal Register](https://cftc.gov/foia/fedreg00/foi000310a.htm)). A Massachusetts consent order and a **$500K** fine against Dub Advisors over its "copy trade" portfolio advertising show that copy products get treated as discretionary management ([MA consent order](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/securities/download/2025-0072-Dub-Advisors-Consent-Order.pdf)). Non-custodial wallet software is generally not money transmission under FinCEN's 2019 guidance ([Katten](https://katten.com/files/646592_2019_06_26_frm_fs_fincen_publishes_guidance_pertaining_to__certain_business_models_involving_convertible_virtual_currencies.pdf)). That helps with custody questions but not with adviser, CTA or CASP questions.

Enforcement also targets the creators themselves. ASIC sent warning notices to **18 finfluencers**, including those inviting people to "copy their trades", in a joint action with eight other regulators in June 2025 ([SMS Magazine](https://smsmagazine.com.au/news/2025/06/12/asic-moves-against-18-finfluencers/)). The SEC and CFTC have targeted AI-trading claims. The CFTC warns that promised returns from "AI bots" are a red flag ([CFTC advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)), and an unregistered adviser was charged in January 2026 over false AI-trading claims ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/sec-charges-unregistered-adviser-with-fraud-over-fake-credentials-false-ai-trading-claims/264923)). The defensible launch posture, which still needs counsel, has six parts. Copies are user-confirmed by default, and auto-copy is an explicit opt-in bounded by limits the follower sets in their own wallet. Every follower sees identical, non-personalised strategy information. Fee splits are disclosed on every copy. Creators pass KYC before their first payout, because they are the paid party. Simulated results are always labelled hypothetical. And Tribe launches only in jurisdictions counsel clears, which in practice means geofencing the US, UK and EU retail until Tribe either complies with or partners into those regimes. Staying spot-only also matters. Leveraged or perpetual products would push US exposure toward the CFTC. One question this research could not settle is whether a creator who earns fees by running a public bot is an "adviser" in any of these regimes. That uncertainty is a reason to keep creators publishing a strategy rather than managing anyone's account.

## The recommendation: positioning, segments, pricing and MVP

**Positioning.** Lead with *"Publish your strategy. CopyGuard keeps it honest."* For followers, the matching line is *"Copy people and bots whose every trade has a receipt, a stop, and a guard."* Bybit's "human vs bot" framing ([Investing.com](https://investing.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/human-vs-bot-bybit-announces-copy-trading-competition-with-200000-usdt-in-prizes-3745854)) fits Tribe's separate Manual and Bot records well and can become a season format. Three things should stay out of all marketing: return promises, "AI trades for you" claims, and paid posts. Regulators target the first two, and X revoked API access for pay-to-post apps in January 2026 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/354736/elon-musks-x-bans-access-infofi-crypto-projects-ai-slop-backlash)).

**Target segments.** Creators come first, because exchanges run at roughly **1 creator per 4–20 copiers** ([Coin Bureau](https://www.coinbureau.com/review/bitget-copy-trading-review)). Recruit in this order. First, Base-native traders who already trade in public on Farcaster, Base App or X and want a verifiable record. Second, systematic hobbyists who want a no-code bot but distrust custodial Telegram bots. Third, Bankr and Fomo power users who are used to social trading on Base but have no creator payout there. On the follower side, the core segment is small-ticket retail copying **$5–$500** per trade, the range the /bot page already uses. Bitget's data shows profitable copiers follow **more than 7 leaders** on average ([Bitget report](https://www.bitget.com/blog/articles/bitget-report-44-percent-of-copy-traders-are-gen-z)), so "copy a basket" should come soon after launch. Memecoin snipers are deliberately excluded, because the evidence says copiers there lose by construction.

**Pricing summary.**

| Item | Recommendation | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Copier fee | One all-in per-copy fee, shown in $ before confirm; no subscription; target the majors band | Phantom 0.05%, MetaMask 0.1%, Fomo 0.5% spot / 0.05% majors; TG bots ~1% |
| Creator share | ~30% of Tribe's fee on copied orders at launch, rising to 40–50% by quality tier | Binance 10% fee commission; exchange profit shares 10% → 30% |
| Outcome hold-back | 50% of creator share held per season and released by copier net outcome; zero on CopyGuard-paused copies | ESMA conflict guidance; Bitget/OKX loss netting |
| Referral | 25% of the referred copier's fees, tapering (e.g. 30/20/10) | Fomo 25%; BONKbot 30/20/10 |
| Performance fee | Not at launch; later ≤10% with per-copier high-water mark | Hyperliquid 10%; Drift per-depositor HWM |
| Creator stake | ≥$100 and ≥5% of the bot's average copied exposure | Hyperliquid 100 USDC / 5% |

**MVP scope.** The build reuses what exists and closes the two gaps in the brief: manual trades currently need Ask Cat, and the publish wizard does not start a real bot.

| Component | In the MVP | Deliberately out |
|---|---|---|
| Manual trade ticket | Form with asset (allowlist), size, required stop, optional take-profit, one-line thesis. Ask Cat can pre-fill it from "Buy $50 ETH with 5% stop", but the user always confirms on the ticket. Posts as a receipt card with post-to-position timing | Leverage, unlisted tokens, editing a thesis after the fill |
| Bot builder | Three templates that fork the existing CRE strategy (e.g. trend-follow, scheduled accumulate, breakout), with parameter slots for assets, size, trades/day (1–10), stop (required), take-profit, and a max-drawdown auto-stop | Code upload, custom indicators, TradingView webhooks (v2) |
| Bot wallet | Runs only in the creator's Privy policy wallet: Flash routes only, per-trade and daily caps, no transfers; creator funds it (stake rule) | Running bots in MetaMask; pooled vaults |
| Incubation and publish | Bot trades privately for 14 days; the wizard then runs the existing eligibility checks against the bot's own record and publishes the running bot | Publishing backtests or simulated records |
| Follower copy | Existing pipeline: confirm per copy for MetaMask, opt-in auto-copy within wallet-enforced caps for Privy, own stop per copy, CopyGuard pause for everyone | Forced sync of follower parameters |
| Creator dashboard | Realised earnings, held-back balance, copier count and retention, copier net outcome, copy gap, Guard history | Notional "volume generated" as the headline number |
| Copy ledger and alerts | For every creator or bot trade: copied, skipped, declined or paused, with reason and both fills; push or Telegram alert | — |
| Disclosures | Per-copy fee and creator cut; "hypothetical" labels; dynamic share of copiers in profit per creator | — |

The riskiest assumption to test first is whether creators will fund a Privy policy wallet for their bot. If they will not, the fallback is manual-trade publishing only, plus "fork the house bot" for followers. That still delivers the receipt-card and CopyGuard story.

## A launch that costs less than one exchange tournament

Exchanges spend **100,000–300,000 USDT** per copy-trading tournament ([AAP/Cision on Bybit Master Trader Arena](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20251117ae25988)), but the cheapest mechanics in their playbooks work at Tribe's scale. Bitget ran a promotion open only to verified elites with **fewer than 5 copiers** ([Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603882773)). Bybit splits each team's prize **50% to the master trader and 50% to followers** by volume, and requires at least 20 active followers to qualify ([AAP/Cision](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260429ae46583/)). Its title-only "View Masters" league cost about 1,000 USDT ([AAP/Cision](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20251114ae24847)). Binance drove trial with a **20,000 USDC** pool paying 1 USDC per mock portfolio that reached +5% ([Binance](https://www.binance.com/en-AE/support/announcement/detail/2431be3394b348dfa90d4e3658335ac5)). Fomo's founders say "the product itself is the only marketing" and that users flooded in once Apple Pay removed onboarding friction ([Alea Research](https://alearesearch.io/podcast-digest/empire/fomo-co-founders-on-building-the-social-network-for-finance)). Four metrics decide whether the launch worked. Creators with at least 5 active copiers (target 15 or more by the end of Season 1), copier 30-day retention, the share of copiers net positive after fees, and realised creator payouts. Copied volume is a diagnostic only and should not be a goal, because optimising it rebuilds the churn incentive the pricing is designed to remove. Three things to avoid in the plan: points with implied airdrops, which draw farmers ([eco.com on Hyperliquid](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15039718-hyperliquid-airdrop-what-happened-and-what-s-next)); any payment to creators for posts, which makes those posts promotions in the UK; and loss-protection offers like Bitget's first-trade cover ([Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603887459)), which would need counsel before Tribe copies them.

## Conclusion

The research shifts where Creator Studio's value lies. It is not in the bot builder, which Bitget made a one-click commodity, or in creator payouts, which are small at Tribe's scale and are the regulators' favourite target. It is in a guarantee that scales with the number of creators. Every published strategy, human or bot, runs inside wallet-enforced limits. A shared guard can pause it for everyone. And its pay depends on what copiers actually made. This makes CopyGuard the payout engine as well as the safety feature, and that turns the main regulatory liability (volume-based creator pay) into the main selling point. No exchange or Telegram bot can match that combination today.

The open bet is creator supply. Every other model in the evidence either pays creators well on performance (exchanges, vaults) or ignores them and wins on distribution (Fomo). Tribe is trying a third path: modest, outcome-gated fee shares plus status and reach. Nothing in the research proves this works. Season 1 should be run as an experiment on that single question, with the founding cohort's retention and copier outcomes deciding whether to raise the creator share, add a capped performance fee, or narrow Creator Studio to manual publishing.
