# Trust, Failure Modes and Regulatory Risk for Creator Copy Trading and Bots (Tribe)

Research date: 2026-11-01. This is not legal advice. Platform rules and figures change often, so each one is dated where the source gives a date. Some primary documents (the full ESMA briefing PDF, FCA PS23/6, the Advisers Act text) could not be read directly in this session, so I relied on law-firm or regulator summaries. Those points are flagged.

## 1. Trust and adoption drivers: track records, metrics, risk controls, skin in the game, and evidence on copier outcomes

### Takeaway
Established platforms build trust with three things. They gate who can be copied (risk-score caps, minimum stakes, a single leader account). They normalize or score risk so traders are compared on skill rather than leverage (Darwinex). And they give copiers controls such as copy stop-loss and minimum sizes, while warning prominently that performance is not guaranteed. Academic evidence suggests copy trading increases herding and risk-taking. I found no rigorous field study measuring the share of copiers who lose money.

### Cited Findings
**Skin in the game and leader gating**
- Hyperliquid (official docs, current as of the 2026 search) pays vault leaders a 10% profit share. Leaders must keep a minimum stake (community docs say at least 5% of the vault) and cannot withdraw below it — [Hyperliquid Docs: for vault leaders](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/for-vault-leaders); [Hyperliquid community docs](https://hyperliquid-co.gitbook.io/community-docs/architecture/hypercore/vault)
- A third-party source says the 10% applies only to profits above a high-water mark. The official docs found here do not say so, so this is unconfirmed — [web3-ethereum-defi docs](https://web3-ethereum-defi.readthedocs.io/vaults/hyperliquid/index.html)
- Bitget elite traders must not follow other elite traders and may hold only one elite trader account. To appear on the spot leaderboard a trader needs at least 100 USDT in spot, a closed elite trade and one enabled copy pair — [Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603777069)
- Bitget's profit share is tiered: Bronze and Silver 10%, Gold 12%, Platinum 14%, Extraordinary 15%, Legend 20%. An older version listed Silver at 11%. Profit share is paid only on profitable orders. The TraderPro program uses a 10% share and requires a 10,000 USDT sub-account — [Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603777069); [Bitget TraderPro guide](https://www.bitget.cloud/academy/guide-to-master-bitget-copy-trade-traderpro-program)
- eToro Popular Investors must keep a capped risk score. eToro's own pages give conflicting thresholds: an older announcement says 6 or lower for Popular Investors and 7 for social traders, and a later page cites a daily risk score of 8 or more over 2 months as a breach — [eToro blog: PI program update](https://www.etoro.com/blog/from-etoro/important-update-regarding-popular-investor-program); [eToro Popular Investor](https://etoro.com/popular-investor); [Finance Magnates: eToro updates social trading requirements](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/products/etoro-updates-social-trading-requirements/)

**Risk normalization and metrics**
- Darwinex runs a "Risk Engine" between the underlying strategy and the investable DARWIN. It holds every DARWIN to the same target, a maximum of 6.5% monthly VaR at 95% confidence, adjusted dynamically between 3.25% and 6.5% — [Darwinex Risk Manager](https://help.darwinex.com/risk-manager); [Darwinex Zero risk engine](https://darwinexzero.document360.io/docs/risk-engine)
- Darwinex scores strategies on 12 "Investable Attributes", including Risk Adjustment (how much the engine has to intervene), Performance (risk-adjusted return compared with 10,000 random strategies at the same risk) and Risk Stability — [Darwinex investable attributes](https://help.darwinex.com/what-are-investable-attributes); [Risk adjustment attribute](https://help.darwinex.com/risk-adjustment-attribute); [Risk stability attribute](https://help.darwinex.com/risk-stability-attribute)

**Copier-side controls and disclosures (eToro CopyTrader risk page, retrieved 2026-11-01)**
- eToro offers Copy Stop Loss but does not guarantee fills at the stop level, and copiers "may lose more than the amount" used to copy. Copied trades below the minimum trade amount are not opened. Risk scores and historical stats are "not a guarantee of future performance". The CFD warning says 55% of retail accounts lose money. eToro describes social features as informational and says its staff are not investment advisers — [eToro copytrading risks](https://www.etoro.com/customer-service/copytrading-risks/)
- eToro's retail-loss percentage moves over time. ASIC quoted 77% in its August 2023 lawsuit. A BrokerRank compilation lists eToro at 51%, the lowest of 49 brokers, against Plus500 at 82%. A "61%" figure appears only on an affiliate page and is unreliable — [The Block: ASIC sues eToro (2023-08-02)](https://www.theblock.co/post/242890/australian-regulator-sues-etoro-over-high-risk-leveraged-derivative-product); [BrokerRank retail loss rates](https://brokerrank.net/research/retail-loss-rates)
- eToro gives affiliates a "dynamic disclaimer percent" tool, which shows the warning is meant to be updated periodically rather than fixed — [eToro Partners PDF](https://etoropartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Dynamic-disclaimer-percent-tool.pdf)

**Academic evidence on outcomes and behaviour**
- Gemayel and Preda (European Journal of Finance, 2018) find that social trading's "scopic" (visible) environment produces excess herding that persists at much higher levels than in traditional environments — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:eurjfi:v:24:y:2018:i:14:p:1144-1175)
- Apesteguia, Oechssler and Weidenholzer (lab experiment) find that copy trading leads to more risk-taking and lower ex-ante welfare, partly because successful leaders may simply have taken more risk. The authors note this is lab evidence only — [CEPR VoxEU](https://cepr.org/article/copy-trading-platforms-and-risk-taking); [BSE Focus](https://focus.bse.eu/copy-trading-risk-taking/)
- Pelster and Hofmann (Journal of Banking & Finance, 2018) find that eToro leaders show a stronger disposition effect, which they attribute to fear of losing followers — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:94:y:2018:i:c:p:75-88)
- Followers on eToro tend to take more risk and to overreact when signal providers raise risk. Doering and Jonen (2018) find that signal providers face convex incentives and increase risk near their high-water mark (cited in 2022 and 2023 papers) — [Review of Managerial Science 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6); [Scientific Reports 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-023-49648-3)
- A lab study by Chmura, Le and Nguyen (JEBO, 2022) examines herding toward leading traders. I could not retrieve its results — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/216773/1/1-s2.0-S016726812200316X-main.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Tribe, the onchain track record is itself the "verified" history. The trust gap shifts to whether that history is representative (survivorship, cherry-picked wallets, side wallets; see section 2) and whether it is risk-adjusted. A Darwinex-style normalized risk view, or at least visible max drawdown, volatility or VaR, would address the leverage-chasing problem shown in the academic work.
- A Hyperliquid-style minimum creator stake in their own strategy or bot is a credible, cheap "skin in the game" rule. The convex-incentive findings (Doering and Jonen) suggest fee designs paid only above a high-water mark, or tied to copier P&L rather than volume, reduce risk-taking incentives. A pure share of trading fees rewards turnover, which is a known conflict.
- Copier-side guardrails such as per-copy stop or drawdown limits, minimum sizes and max allocation per creator are industry standard, so a launch without them would look substandard.

### Gaps
- I found no credible field statistic for "X% of copiers lose money" on a copy-trading product specifically, as opposed to CFD accounts generally. The eToro figures cover all CFD accounts.
- I did not verify Binance's or Collective2's lead-trader rules (minimum history, AUM caps, skin-in-the-game) in this session.
- No rigorous study quantifies survivorship bias in leaderboards. It is widely asserted but I found no sourced number.

## 2. Failure modes and scandals: fake PnL, wash trading, front-running copiers, bot and AI-agent exploits, and the controls that followed

### Takeaway
In onchain copy trading the dominant risks are adversarial leaders and infrastructure compromise. Leaders front-run or dump on copiers in thin tokens, or groom attractive track records before exit scams. Infrastructure incidents include Telegram bot servers and AI-agent dashboards being compromised. The controls that followed were refunds from treasury, server and key rotation, 2FA, transfer delays, and per-period caps and liquidity checks for copiers.

### Cited Findings
- **Banana Gun (Telegram trading bot), 19 Sep 2024.** About $3M was drained from 11 users through unauthorized transfers. The team pointed to a vulnerability in the "Telegram message oracle", shut the bot down, moved to new servers, redeployed the backend and pledged full refunds from treasury without selling tokens. One outlet reports 2FA and transfer delays were added — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/318074/banana-gun-exploit-refund); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/banana-gun-to-refund-dollar3-million-following-exploit-of-telegram-bot); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/banana-gun-crypto-bot-refunds-3m). I did not confirm that refunds were paid.
- **AIXBT (AI agent on Base), 18 Mar 2025.** An attacker got into the agent's dashboard at about 02:00 UTC and queued two prompts that made the agent send about 55 ETH (roughly $100K–106K) from its "simulacrum" wallet. The maintainer said the agent was not manipulated; the dashboard was the weak point. The team migrated servers, swapped keys and paused dashboard access, and the AIXBT token fell about 20% — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/346911/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/310510/aixbt-ai-influencer-hacked-100k-ethereum); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hacker-breaches-ai-crypto-bot-aixbt-steals-55-eth)
- **Structural front-running.** The leader always fills before followers, and the gap widens with more followers and thinner liquidity. A large copied position in a thin token can push the price up and leave followers without exit liquidity — [Cielo docs: risks](https://docs.cielo.finance/guides/copy-trading/risks-and-considerations); [KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/what-is-copy-trading) (vendor sources)
- **Groomed wallets and side-wallet schemes.** Bad actors run pump-and-dumps knowing copiers track them. One tactic is to accumulate a token in an unlinked side wallet, buy from the main wallet so copiers follow, then sell. Wallets also build attractive patterns to lure copiers before exit scams — [Arkham research (Aug 2026)](https://info.arkm.com/research/crypto-copy-trading-what-is-it-and-how-to-do-it); [Cielo docs](https://docs.cielo.finance/guides/copy-trading/risks-and-considerations)
- An arXiv preprint (2026) on meme-coin copy trading describes adversaries deploying manipulative bots to front-run trades, hide positions and fake sentiment. It says no robust defensive framework exists — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08641?context=cs)
- **Mitigations proposed by vendors:** check a leader's 24h volume in the tokens they trade, keep leader capital in line with pair liquidity, spread allocations across uncorrelated leaders, and cap how much a copied wallet can buy per period — [Cielo docs](https://docs.cielo.finance/guides/copy-trading/risks-and-considerations); [Arkham](https://info.arkm.com/research/crypto-copy-trading-what-is-it-and-how-to-do-it)
- **US enforcement on fake performance claims.** In a January 2026 SEC case, an unregistered person was charged with fraud over fake credentials and false AI-trading claims. In a March 2026 action, Dub Advisors paid a $500K fine plus restitution over ads for "copy trade" portfolios of well-known figures. The Massachusetts Securities Division consent order is on file; Law360 does not name the regulator — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/sec-charges-unregistered-adviser-with-fraud-over-fake-credentials-false-ai-trading-claims/264923); [MA Securities Division consent order 2025-0072](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/securities/download/2025-0072-Dub-Advisors-Consent-Order.pdf); [Law360 (2026-03-30)](https://law360.com/articles/2459415)
- **Cherry-picking.** The SEC continues to fine advisers for allocating winning trades to favoured accounts, for example a $750K penalty — [Law360](https://law360.com/articles/1881103); [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/sec-targets-investment-adviser-cherry-1519904)

### Inferences
- Tribe's design (Chainlink CRE "CopyGuard", non-custodial, Base) maps directly onto these failure modes. Useful guards include per-token liquidity and market-cap minimums, per-period copy caps, slippage limits, and delaying or blocking copies when the leader's own buy is a large share of pool depth. Detecting linked or side wallets and funding-source clustering helps against groomed track records.
- The AIXBT and Banana Gun cases show that the key-management and dashboard or bot backend is the real attack surface, not the model. Session keys or spend permissions scoped to specific contracts and amounts, plus 2FA or delays on withdrawals and changes to permissions, are the controls the market converged on.
- Fee-share-on-volume gives creators an incentive to churn and to trade illiquid tokens their copiers then push up. That is the front-running conflict in fee form.

### Gaps
- I found no well-documented case of onchain wash trading used specifically to fake a copy-trading leaderboard track record, though it is widely described as a risk.
- I found no documented case of a centralized exchange (Binance or Bitget) banning lead traders for faked PnL in this session.

## 3. Regulation: EU (MiFID II, MiCA), UK (FCA), US (SEC, CFTC, FinCEN), and when fee-sharing creators become advisers

### Takeaway
In the EU, ESMA treats copy trading case by case as portfolio management, investment advice, or reception and transmission of orders. Since its MiCA Q&A, the same classification logic applies to crypto-asset services. Paying copied traders based on followers or AUM is an inducement and conflict-of-interest issue the platform must manage. In the UK, crypto promotions to retail have been tightly regulated since 8 Oct 2023, and finfluencers with any commercial interest can fall inside the perimeter. In the US, signal and automated-strategy services risk CTA (CFTC) or investment adviser (SEC) status unless they fit narrow publisher-style exclusions. Non-custodial wallet software is generally not money transmission under FinCEN's 2019 guidance, though this is contested at the margins.

### Cited Findings
**EU: MiFID II, ESMA Supervisory Briefing on Copy Trading (ESMA35-42-1428, 30 Mar 2023)**
- Copy trading may be portfolio management, investment advice, or reception and transmission of orders or provision of signals, depending on the firm-client relationship, case by case. It builds on a 2012 ESMA Q&A (Question 9) on automatic execution of trade signals — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/200553); [ESMA briefing PDF](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf); [PwC Legal summary (2023-08-03)](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services)
- Where the service is portfolio management or advice, the copied traders need the required knowledge and competence. Firms should select copied traders carefully, verify their credentials, and review them periodically against standards — [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services)
- **Remuneration of copied traders.** Firms should have a remuneration policy for copied traders where the MiFID II rules apply. Variable criteria (number and value of trades copied, followers, AUM) must not put copied traders ahead of clients. Where those rules do not apply, inducement rules still do and payments must be disclosed. Copied traders may count as "relevant persons", and as MAR "experts" if they present as financial experts making recommendations — [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services)
- Conflicts arise from payment structures and from copied traders also being clients. Marketing, including past performance, must be fair, clear and not misleading, and explain how performance is calculated. Suitability applies for advice or portfolio management (with a suitability report before each advised transaction), and appropriateness applies for RTO. Copied trades should be checked against client risk limits — [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services); [MFSA notice](https://www.mfsa.mt/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/The-European-Securities-and-Markets-Authority-ESMA-has-issued-a-Supervisory-Briefing-on-Copy-Trading-Services-Assessing-the-Application-of-the-Relevant-MiFID-II-Rules.pdf)
- ESMA separately issued a supervisory briefing on algorithmic trading under MiFID II on 26 Feb 2026 — [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/228346); [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/esma-publishes-supervisory-briefing-on-algorithmic-trading-in-the-eu)

**EU: MiCA**
- ESMA Q&A 2463 says MiCA does not define "auto trading" or "copy trading". CASPs must identify which crypto-asset service(s) they provide case by case. The MiFID II copy-trading guidance (2012 Q&A 9 and sections 2.1–2.2 of the 2023 briefing) applies mutatis mutandis, but only for that classification. MiCA advice and portfolio management are read consistently with their MiFID II equivalents. The page gives no publication date — [MiCA Q&A 2463 mirror](https://micawtf.pages.dev/eu-level/q-and-a/esma-qa-2463-auto-trading/)
- MiCA Art. 81 (advice and portfolio management) requires suitability assessments refreshed at least every two years, risk warnings, suitability reports for advice and periodic statements for portfolio management. It bars portfolio managers from keeping fees or benefits from issuers, offerors and similar parties — [ESMA Interactive Single Rulebook, Art. 81](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-81-providing-advice-crypto-assets)
- ESMA Guidelines on suitability and periodic statements under MiCA (ESMA35-1872330276-2031, March 2025) cover Art. 81(1), (7), (8), (10)–(12) and (14) — [ESMA Guidelines PDF](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-03/ESMA35-1872330276-2031_Guidelines_on_suitability_and_periodic_statement_MiCA_DE.pdf); [CONSOB copy](https://www.consob.it/documents/d/asset-library-1912910/esma35-1872330276-2031)

**UK: FCA**
- Crypto financial promotions regime: PS23/6 was published 8 Jun 2023 and took effect 8 Oct 2023. Cryptoassets are "Restricted Mass Market Investments". It brings a 24-hour cooling-off period for first-time investors, personalised risk warnings, client categorisation and appropriateness checks, and a ban on incentives to invest. It applies to overseas firms marketing to UK consumers. Non-Handbook guidance FG23/3 was finalised 2 Nov 2023 — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/fg23-3-finalised-non-handbook-guidance-cryptoasset-financial-promotions); [Dechert](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2023/6/fca-issues-final-rules-for-marketing-cryptoassets-in-the-uk.html); [Hogan Lovells](https://engage.hoganlovells.com/knowledgeservices/news/uk-fca-introduces-new-rules-for-marketing-cryptoassets-to-uk-consumers)
- Finfluencers: FG24/1 (26 Mar 2024) replaced FG15/4. It sets no new obligations but explains when unauthorised influencers' posts are financial promotions. "Any commercial interest", including affiliate or referral commission, can make a post "in the course of business". Memes can be promotions, and truncation such as "see more" must not hide warnings. Nine finfluencers were charged in May 2024, and 20 were interviewed under caution in Oct 2024 — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uk-fca-finalises-social-media-financial-promotions-guidance); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/fca-publishes-finalised-guidance-on-financial-promotions-on-social-media); [Freshfields](https://riskandcompliance.freshfields.com/post/102jmhr/fca-takes-further-steps-to-crackdown-on-finfluencers-following-issuing-guidance)

**US: CFTC (CTA/CPO)**
- The CFTC publisher exclusion traces to *Lowe v. SEC* (1985). After a 1999 court decision struck down an earlier rule, the CFTC amended it. A publisher still loses the exemption if it directs client accounts or gives advice tailored to a client's circumstances — [CFTC Federal Register, 10 Mar 2000](https://cftc.gov/foia/fedreg00/foi000310a.htm); [RCFP](https://www.rcfp.org/journals/the-news-media-and-the-law-spring-2000/cftc-backs-rule-would-requi/)
- The CFTC has brought actions against unregistered sellers of trading systems and signals. In the SignalPush (Barelt Media) case the firm offered trade signals plus automated trading on binary-options platforms. Commentators flag paid Discord channels with algorithmic perp signals as CTA risk and warn that publisher and educator exemptions are read narrowly and case by case — [Bitcoin.com: crypto KOLs and CTA risk](https://news.bitcoin.com/are-you-a-commodity-trading-advisor-crypto-kols-at-risk/); [Blank Rome: CTAs, trading signals and DeFi](https://blankrome.com/publications/ctas-trading-signals-and-defi-connecting-dots-or-unconnected-leaps-logic) (the Blank Rome page returned HTTP 429, so its content is not verified); [Nat Law Review: trading screens and CTAs](https://natlawreview.com/article/does-cftc-require-trading-screens-and-technology-providers-to-register-ctas)
- In August 2024 a judge dismissed a case against Seeking Alpha, which commentators read as relevant to publisher protections for financial information — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/securities/1510364/judge-dismisses-case-against-seeking-alpha-implications-for-publishers-of-financial-information)

**US: SEC and state adviser rules**
- A "copy trade" portfolio product (Dub Advisors, an SEC-registered adviser) is regulated as discretionary management. Clients who copy a portfolio have their allocated assets managed by replicating trades. A state consent order and a $500K fine (reported 30 Mar 2026) followed over its advertising — [MA consent order](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/securities/download/2025-0072-Dub-Advisors-Consent-Order.pdf); [Law360](https://law360.com/articles/2459415)
- In 2023 the SEC fined an adviser $250K for paying more than 200 influencers and newsletter providers over $8M for referrals without the required solicitation disclosures — [RIA Compliance Consultants](https://www.ria-compliance-consultants.com/tag/sec/page/2/)

**US: FinCEN and money transmission**
- FinCEN's 2019 CVC guidance (FIN-2019-G001, 9 May 2019) says unhosted (non-custodial) wallet software providers are not money transmitters. This also holds for multisig providers that need a second user key, unless they conflate the service with hosted custody. Hosted wallet providers are money transmitters. The guidance is interpretive, and its boundary was contested in the Samourai Wallet prosecution — [Katten](https://katten.com/files/646592_2019_06_26_frm_fs_fincen_publishes_guidance_pertaining_to__certain_business_models_involving_convertible_virtual_currencies.pdf); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2019/06/fincen-consolidates-guidance); [Coin Center](https://coincenter.org/fincens-new-cryptocurrency-guidance-matches-coin-center-recommendations/)
- A single unverified source says FinCEN withdrew its December 2020 unhosted-wallet proposed rule in October 2026, with the 2019 guidance still governing. Check the Federal Register before relying on this — [Zyphe](https://www.zyphe.com/resources/news/fincen-unhosted-wallet-rule-withdrawn-october-2026)

### Inferences
- **EU.** If Tribe auto-executes creators' trades in users' wallets under a standing mandate, that looks most like portfolio management (or advice plus RTO) under MiCA via Q&A 2463. That means CASP authorisation and Art. 81 suitability. A model where each copy is user-confirmed may be argued as RTO or no service, but this is uncertain. Whether "fully decentralised" provision falls outside MiCA (Recital 22) is unclear for an app with a front end, fee collection and creator onboarding. I did not research this here.
- **Creators sharing fees.** In the EU the briefing treats payments to copied traders based on followers, trades copied or AUM as remuneration or inducement and a conflict to be managed and disclosed. Creators may also be "relevant persons" or MAR "experts". In the US, a creator paid per copier-generated fee who effectively directs trading in followers' accounts fails the core publisher-exclusion condition (not directing accounts). That is a strong indicator of CTA risk (for derivatives or perps) or investment adviser risk (if the tokens are securities), alongside platform-level risk. My background knowledge, not verified here, is that the Advisers Act defines an adviser as anyone who, for compensation, advises others on securities as a business. Whether a creator "bot" counts as advice is unresolved.
- **US product scope.** Perp or leveraged trading (Definitive and others) pushes toward CFTC jurisdiction. Spot tokens push toward the SEC only if the token is a security. Non-custodial design mainly helps on money transmission (FinCEN 2019), not on adviser, CTA or CASP questions.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full ESMA briefing text (the PDF was unparseable in this environment) or the FCA PS23/6 rule text. Paragraph-level citations are missing.
- I found no 2025–2026 CFTC or SEC enforcement aimed squarely at onchain or non-custodial copy-trading apps.
- The FCA has no copy-trading-specific statement for crypto in the sources I found. The FCA's ongoing UK cryptoasset regime (rules expected 2026) was not researched here.
- US state money-transmission treatment of non-custodial apps that take a fee cut was not researched state by state.
- Changes to US federal crypto market-structure law in 2025–2026 (for example, any CLARITY Act status) were not checked. They could materially change SEC and CFTC allocation.

## 4. Practical compliance patterns used by copy-trading apps

### Takeaway
The common pattern is to describe social features as informational and not advice, publish prominent and periodically refreshed loss and past-performance warnings, give copiers stop-loss and minimum-size controls, gate and periodically re-review leaders, and run licensed entities per jurisdiction (eToro). Crypto-native apps also lean on non-custodial design and geo-restrictions.

### Cited Findings
- eToro states its social trading features are informational and its employees are not investment advisers. It discloses that crypto clients lack ICF, FSCS and FOS protection because crypto is outside MiFID, and lists separate licensed entities (CySEC 109/10, FCA, ASIC, FSAS Seychelles, ADGM FSRA) — [eToro copytrading risks](https://www.etoro.com/customer-service/copytrading-risks/)
- Retail-loss disclosure for CFDs is mandatory and percentages are refreshed periodically (eToro shows 55% on the current page, 51%–77% across sources and dates). The figure counts accounts that lost money, not the amount lost — [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/customer-service/copytrading-risks/); [BrokerRank](https://brokerrank.net/research/retail-loss-rates); [BrokerChooser](https://brokerchooser.com/nl/broker-reviews/etoro-review/cfd-risk-warning)
- Standard warnings include "past performance is not an indication of future results", that hypothetical results are prepared with hindsight, and that the copier is responsible for losses from automatic execution — [eToro](https://www.etoro.com/customer-service/copytrading-risks/)
- Platforms rely on the publisher exclusion in their terms by giving identical, non-customized information to all subscribers. This is a self-asserted position, not a regulatory ruling — [Cotes terms](https://cotes-website-b80b.onrender.com/legal/terms.html)
- UK crypto promotions require a 24h cooling-off period, personalised risk warnings and appropriateness tests before a first investment (since 8 Oct 2023) — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/fg23-3-finalised-non-handbook-guidance-cryptoasset-financial-promotions)

### Inferences
- A defensible launch posture for Tribe, which still needs counsel review:
  - Geofence the US, the UK (or comply with the full UK promotions regime) and EU retail (or partner with or become a MiCA CASP).
  - Make every copy user-initiated, with user-set limits.
  - Pay creators only from transparent, disclosed fee splits, ideally based on copier profitability.
  - Show a dynamic, data-backed disclosure of copier outcomes, analogous to eToro's percentage.
  - Require creator stake minimums and run leader-quality reviews.
- KYC tiers matter mainly for creators, because they are paid and are the regulatory pressure point. Light-touch KYC for copiers in non-custodial mode is common, but it is not a legal safe harbour.

### Gaps
- I did not verify which crypto copy-trading apps (Bitget, Binance, Hyperliquid front ends, onchain bots) geo-block the US or UK, or their KYC tier details, from primary terms in this session.
