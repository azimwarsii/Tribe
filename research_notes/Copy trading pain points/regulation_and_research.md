# Copy Trading, Social Trading and Automated Trading Tools: Regulatory Warnings, Enforcement and Academic Evidence on Retail Harms

Research date: 28 October 2026. All items dated. Primary sources preferred; secondary-only items are flagged.

## 1. Regulatory positions and warnings (ESMA, FCA, CySEC, ASIC, SEC/FINRA, CFTC, IOSCO)

### Takeaway
ESMA's 30 March 2023 Supervisory Briefing treats copy trading as regulated MiFID II activity. Automatic copying is "regularly" portfolio management, and manual copying may be investment advice or reception and transmission of orders (RTO). That brings in suitability, rules on lead-trader pay, knowledge-and-competence requirements and limits on past-performance marketing. In May 2025 IOSCO made this a global baseline through its "Online Imitative Trading Practices" good practices. Elsewhere, enforcement has gone mostly after finfluencers (FCA, SEC, FINRA), AI-washing (SEC) and broad CFD target markets (ASIC v eToro), not after copy-trading mechanics as such.

### Cited Findings

**ESMA (EU)**
- ESMA Supervisory Briefing on Copy Trading, ESMA35-42-1428, dated **30 March 2023**, 25 pages. It is not formally binding on national supervisors. — [ESMA PDF](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf); [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/200553); [PwC Legal summary](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/esma-communicates-supervisory-expectations-on-copy-trading-services)
- **Classification.** The briefing says services that "automatically execute" third-party trade signals, with no client intervention, qualify as **portfolio management**. Footnote 22 says that clicking "autocopy" or "auto-follow" buttons "effectively are the mandates to the firm". Where the client must act before execution, the service may be **investment advice** (personal recommendations) or **RTO/execution**. ESMA adds that the copied traders who share their trades may themselves be giving investment advice. ESMA's overall conclusion is that copy trading services "are likely to either fall under portfolio management or investment advice" (para. 71). — [ESMA35-42-1428, paras 27–33, 71](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Suitability.** Supervisors are told to ask whether firms ensure "the client's and the copied trader's risk profiles are not defined too broadly", whether firms monitor copied transactions against the client's risk limits and intervene, and whether firms have "(IT) arrangements in place to ensure that unsuitable transactions are not automatically copied". They should also check how firms stop a copied trader who switches into instruments the copier lacks the knowledge for, whether firms run an "ex-ante evaluation" of people whose trades become copyable, and whether firms set "limits or other restrictions on the trading activity" of copied traders. Where the service is investment advice, a suitability report is due before each transaction. — [ESMA35-42-1428, section 2.5](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Appropriateness (RTO model).** Before executing a copied order, the firm must check that the instrument matches the copier's knowledge and experience, warn the copier if it does not, and record the warning (paras 59–62). — [ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Lead-trader pay and conflicts (section 2.6).** ESMA names conflicts arising from "the structure of the payment made to the traders whose trades are being copied". It also notes that a copied trader may pay the firm as a client while being paid to share trades. Supervisors are to check whether variable pay is based on "Number and value of trades copied; Number of followers of their strategy; Performance of the average AUM". Commission-based payments to lead traders may count as **inducements**, which must be disclosed to copiers. — [ESMA35-42-1428, paras 63–69](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Lead-trader qualifications (section 2.7).** Copied traders "would need to have the required knowledge and competence" for advice or portfolio management, and firms need procedures to make sure they do. Firms should also check that copied traders disclose interests and conflicts under MAR Art. 20. — [ESMA35-42-1428, paras 70–72](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Marketing and past performance.** Benefits must have "the same prominence as the associated risks". Past performance "should not be the most prominent element" and should cover the preceding **5 years**, or at least a complete 12-month period. Simulated past performance is barred from future-performance claims. Gross figures must disclose the effect of fees. Costs must be disclosed ex ante. — [ESMA35-42-1428, section 2.3](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **ESMA advice briefing (July 2023).** A separate supervisory briefing on the definition of investment advice says copied traders may be giving investment advice to their copiers, depending on how the service is designed. — [Freshfields summary](https://riskandcompliance.freshfields.com/post/102ik46/understanding-the-definition-of-investment-advice-under-mifid-esma-revises-13-y); [Dillon Eustace, 1 Aug 2023](https://www.dilloneustace.com/legal-updates/the-definition-of-advice-under-mifid)
- **MiCA.** ESMA has said its MiFID II copy-trading guidance applies equally to copy trading of crypto-assets under MiCA (secondary summary). — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/eu-update-on-mica-implementation-implications-for-copy-trading-services/)

**IOSCO (global)**
- On **19 May 2025** IOSCO published three final reports as wave 3 of its Retail Investor Online Safety Roadmap, which launched in November 2024: (1) the *Final Report on Online Imitative Trading Practices – Copy Trading, Mirror Trading, Social Trading*; (2) a Finfluencers report; (3) a Digital Engagement Practices (DEPs) report. The copy-trading report warns that, because trades are copied automatically, retail investors "may not fully understand the implications of their investments". They may copy strategies that do not fit their ability to bear losses or their risk tolerance, which can cause significant losses, "especially when lead traders take high risks or disclose risks and costs inadequately". — [Mondo Visione/IOSCO press release, 19 May 2025](https://mondovisione.com/news/iosco-publishes-final-reports-on-finfluencers-online-imitative-trading-practice-2025519); [IOSCO news PDF](https://www.iosco.org/news/pdf/IOSCONEWS768.pdf)
- IOSCO good practices for intermediaries: assess whether the copy-trading service amounts to investment advice, individual portfolio management or another trading service; assess whether lead-trader pay structures create conflicts of interest; set procedures for selecting and **removing** lead traders; and regularly review both lead traders' conduct and **copy traders' outcomes** on the platform. — [IOSCO report (IOSCOPD793)](https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD793.pdf); [CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/int/regulatory-news/iosco-online-imitative-trading-practices-copy-trading-mirror-trading-social-trading)
- The Finfluencers report flags gaps for "unregistered individuals who influence retail investors without the professional qualifications or oversight" of registered advisers. The DEPs report says DEPs used to deliver advice should not "prioritize the interests of market intermediaries over those of retail investors". — [Mondo Visione, 19 May 2025](https://mondovisione.com/news/iosco-publishes-final-reports-on-finfluencers-online-imitative-trading-practice-2025519)
- IOSCO published a separate Neo-Brokers final report with five recommendations on 12 November 2025. — [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/eu/iosco-final-report-on-neo-brokers/)

**ASIC (Australia)**
- ASIC v eToro Aus Capital Ltd (media release 23-204MR, Aug 2023) is ASIC's **first design-and-distribution-obligations (DDO) case**. ASIC alleges eToro's CFD target market was too broad: it included inexperienced, medium-risk-tolerance clients who did not understand CFDs. It also alleges the screening test was "very difficult to fail": clients could change answers without limit and were **prompted when choosing answers that would cause failure**. Almost **20,000 eToro clients lost money** on CFDs between 5 Oct 2021 and 14 Jun 2023, and eToro's own website said **77%** of retail accounts lose money. Deputy Chair Sarah Court said issuers "cannot simply reverse engineer their target markets to fit existing client bases". The hearing was listed to continue on 27–28 April 2026, and no judgment had been found as of this research. — [ASIC 23-204MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2023-releases/23-204mr-asic-sues-etoro-in-its-first-design-and-distribution-action-to-protect-consumers-from-high-risk-cfd-products/); [ASIC concise statement](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/s2qdjuat/23-204mr-asic-v-etoro-concise-statement.pdf)

It was linked to a **91% fall** in aggregate net retail losses, from A$372m to A$33m per quarter on average. Its stricter option would expand the order to cover copy trading (secondary report). — [Piper Alderman](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/margin-called-asic-to-expand-retail-cfd-restrictions/); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/etoro-dominates-australias-cfd-market-where-trader-loyalty-rarely-survives-a-year/)
- In 2026 ASIC reported nearly A$40m in refunds after a CFD-sector review found more than half the sector fell short of the rules. — [ASIC 26-004MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-004mr-asic-secures-nearly-40-million-in-refunds-to-investors-and-drives-change-after-cfd-sector-falls-short/)

### Inferences
- Under the ESMA and IOSCO frameworks, a product with an "auto-follow" button that executes trades is likely portfolio management in the EU. Lead traders would then be "relevant persons" or inducement recipients, and the operator would own suitability and monitoring of copier outcomes.
- Enforcement so far has targeted *marketing* (finfluencers, AI-washing, misleading ads) and *distribution gating* (ASIC's screening-test case) more than the copy mechanism itself. The ASIC eToro allegations, especially prompting users toward passing answers, are a direct warning against designing onboarding to "nudge through" checks.

- No CySEC circular on copy trading or lead traders was retrieved. CySEC's specific rules (e.g., lead-trader remuneration caps) remain unverified.
- No CFTC advisory specific to *crypto copy trading* was found. The 2024 AI-bot advisory is the closest.
- No 2023–2026 enforcement fine against a crypto exchange specifically for copy-trading conduct (Bybit/Binance/Bitget) was confirmed. Bitget publishes its own anti-abuse rules for copy trading. — [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603897814)
- The full IOSCOPD793 text returned HTTP 403, so the complete good-practice list was not reviewed verbatim.
- The ASIC v eToro outcome or penalty was not confirmed. Secondary reports conflict on status.
- The Mauritius FSC's reported restriction on copy trading for new 2025 licensees is unverified (aggregator only).

## 2. Disclosures and statistics: CFD loss rates, copy-trading performance, complaints

### Takeaway
Regulators' own data show that most retail leveraged traders lose: 74–89% of EU CFD accounts (2018), 68% of Australian retail CFD clients in FY2023-24 (A$458m net loss), and 77% at eToro per its own disclosure cited by ASIC. Credible, independent loss statistics for copy-trading users specifically are almost nonexistent. Exchange-published "X% of copiers profit" figures are marketing.

### Cited Findings
- **ESMA (27 Mar 2018).** National regulators' analyses found **74–89%** of retail CFD accounts typically lose money, with average losses per client of **€1,600 to €29,000**. ESMA introduced leverage caps from 30:1 to 2:1, margin close-out, negative balance protection, limits on incentives and a standardised risk warning giving the firm's percentage of losing accounts. — [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/regulation/esma-to-ban-binary-options-restrict-cfds/)
- **ASIC FY2023-24.** **68%** of retail CFD clients (about 133,674) lost money, with net losses of **A$458m** including A$73m in fees. **85%** of options-CFD clients lost money, and **69%** of 61,424 crypto-settled CFD clients. — [Piper Alderman](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/margin-called-asic-to-expand-retail-cfd-restrictions/); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/etoro-dominates-australias-cfd-market-where-trader-loyalty-rarely-survives-a-year/)
- **ASIC March 2020.** Retail CFD traders lost **A$428m** in the single week of 16–22 March 2020. — [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/retail-cfd-traders-lose-428m-week-16-22-march-2020-asic-analysis-shows/)
- **eToro.** **77%** of retail accounts lost money on CFDs, according to eToro's website as cited by ASIC in 2023. Published eToro percentages vary by entity and period (reports range from 51% to 77%). — [ASIC 23-204MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2023-releases/23-204mr-asic-sues-etoro-in-its-first-design-and-distribution-action-to-protect-consumers-from-high-risk-cfd-products/); [BrokerChooser](https://brokerchooser.com/nl/broker-reviews/etoro-review/cfd-risk-warning)
- **Crypto copy trading, 90-day multi-exchange analysis (Binance, Bybit, MEXC; SECONDARY ONLY).** It reportedly found that **97%** of lead traders were personally profitable but only **43.6%** made profits for followers, with an overall copier win rate of **48.5%**. The original study was not located, and the figure comes via a KuCoin blog, a competitor. Treat it as unverified. — [KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026)
- **Bitget (self-reported).** Claims that 93% of futures and 82% of spot copy traders made profits. This is promotional and unaudited. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/gen-z-dominates-crypto-copy-trading-space-85-make-profit/)

### Inferences
- The standardised "X% of retail accounts lose money" warning is a proven regulatory template. A copy-trading product could adopt a similar audited disclosure covering *copier* accounts (not leaders), net of fees, which would address IOSCO's "review copy traders' outcomes" good practice.
- The gap between lead-trader and follower profitability (if the secondary 97% vs 43.6% figure holds) matches the academic findings in section 3: execution lag, fees, slippage and leaders' convex incentives.

### Gaps
- No regulator-published loss rate for copy-trading users specifically was found (EU, UK, AU or US).
- No FCA, Financial Ombudsman, CFPB or ASIC (AFCA) complaint counts specific to copy trading were found.

## 3. Academic findings: copier vs leader performance, herding, disposition effect, leader incentives, attention, gamification, pump-and-dumps

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed evidence consistently points to harm. Copying past winners does not earn abnormal returns. Copy options increase excessive risk-taking in experiments. Leaders who gain followers become *more* biased (stronger disposition effect), and under convex fee incentives they gamble on lottery-like stocks when far behind or ahead. Followers chase raw past returns and visibility. Attention-driven herding (Robinhood) predicts negative returns. Gamification raises trading and risk, mostly among low-literacy users.

### Cited Findings

**Copier performance**
- **Dorfleitner, Fischer, Lung, Willmertinger, Stang & Dietrich (2018), "To follow or not to follow: An empirical analysis of the returns of actors on social trading platforms," *Quarterly Review of Economics and Finance* 70:160–171.** "Simply investing in those traders with the highest accumulated returns leads to high losses". No strategy shows a positive abnormal return after transaction costs (Carhart four-factor model). Highly active trading is negatively related to returns. Number of followers or invested capital shows **no positive relation** to returns, so there is no wisdom-of-the-crowd effect. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v70y2018icp160-171.html)
- **Röder & Walter (2019), "What Drives Investment Flows into Social Trading Portfolios?" *Journal of Financial Research* 42(2):383–411.** Covers more than 5,300 social trading portfolios (wikifolio structured products). Flows chase past performance, almost exclusively for the top performers. Flows follow **raw returns rather than alphas**, are highly persistent, and are driven by **visibility on the platform** and active trader comments. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfnres/v42y2019i2p383-411.html)

**Copy trading and risk-taking (experimental)**
- **Apesteguia, Oechssler & Weidenholzer (2020), "Copy Trading," *Management Science* 66(12):5608–5622, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2019.3508.** In a lab experiment, information about others' performance pushed subjects toward riskier assets, and the option to copy **amplified** this. The authors conclude copy trading fosters excessive risk-taking. The BSE commentary notes the evidence is lab-based. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:66:y:12:i:2020:p:5608-5622); [BSE Focus](https://dev.focus.bse.eu/copy-trading-risk-taking/)
- **Scientific Reports (2023), s41598-023-49648-3.** An experiment with **807 retail investors** found that upward social comparison (seeing better-performing peers) increased risk-taking. It also summarises prior eToro evidence that followers take more risk and overreact when signal providers take more risk. — [Nature/Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49648-3.pdf)

**Leader behaviour once they have followers**
- **Pelster & Hofmann (2018), "About the fear of reputational loss: Social trading and the disposition effect," *Journal of Banking & Finance* 94:75–88, DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2018.07.003.** A difference-in-differences design on social trading platform data found that becoming a first-time leader **increases the disposition effect** (holding losers, selling winners) in both FX and stocks/indices. Proposed explanations are responsibility to followers, fear of losing followers by admitting mistakes, and image management. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v94y2018icp75-88.html); [Paderborn RIS](https://ris.uni-paderborn.de/record/3520)
- **Oehler & Schneider (2023), *Review of Managerial Science* 17(4), DOI 10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6.** Social trading incentive structures give signal providers very limited monetary downside, which "may motivate traders to gamble". Signal providers at the extremes of relative performance raise their share of **lottery-like stocks**, and underperformers raise net exposure to them, "exposing signal followers to a lottery-like return structure". The paper also cites Doering & Jonen (2018): providers with performance fees and high watermarks face convex incentives and **increase risk near their high watermark**. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/rvmgts/v17y2023i4d10.1007_s11846-022-00560-6.html); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6)
- **Kirchler et al. (2018), *Journal of Finance*.** Tournament incentives increase risk-taking by underperforming traders. This is relevant to leaderboards (cited in Oehler & Schneider 2023). — [Springer (Oehler & Schneider)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-022-00560-6)
- **eToro (2013, company-reported).** eToro claimed that heavily copied traders moved to lower risk levels. This has not been independently verified. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/brokers/etoro-social-network-hits-50-million-trades/)
- **Doering, Neumann & Paul, "A Primer on Social Trading Networks – Institutional Aspects and Empirical Evidence" (SSRN 2291421).** Covers platform institutional design and fee structures. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2291421)
- **MIT thesis (eToro data).** Leaders' style factors and recent gains predict follower gains and losses. Followers spread across several leaders. — [DSpace@MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/112852)
- **ICIS 2019 (forex social trading platform).** Trader consistency speeds follower learning and trader profitability improves it, but faster learning did not guarantee better outcomes. — [AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2019/blockchain_fintech/blockchain_fintech/13)

**Attention, herding and FOMO**
- **Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz (2022), "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users," *Journal of Finance* 77(6):3141–3190.** Robinhood users engage in more attention-induced trading, and outages reduce trading in high-attention stocks. Intense herding episodes predict negative returns: the top stocks bought each day show about **−4.7% abnormal returns over 20 days** in the published version (working paper: −3% / −6% over 5 days). Herding is linked to the app's simplified display of information. — [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:6:p:3141-3190); [SSRN WP PDF](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SSRN-id3715077.pdf)
- **VoxEU/CEPR working paper, "Social media interactions and biases in investment decisions" (2023).** Links social-media interaction to investment biases. — [VoxEU PDF](https://voxeu.org/system/files/2023-02/Social%20media%20interactions%20and%20biases%20in%20investment%20decisions.pdf)

**Gamification**
- **Chapkovski, Khapko & Zoican (2024), "Trading Gamification and Investor Behavior," *Management Science*, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2022.02650.** A randomized online experiment. Lower-literacy investors self-select into hedonic gamified platforms (confetti, badges). Hedonic gamification raises trading volume by about **5%**. About **70%** of the gamified/non-gamified gap comes from self-selection and **30%** from gamification itself. Price-trend alerts reinforce mistakes for investors with wrong beliefs. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/epdf/10.1287/mnsc.2022.02650); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/markets/study-finds-gamified-apps-push-diy-traders-to-make-riskier-investments/)
- **"Gamified risk-taking," *Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance* 46 (2025).** **605 participants** in four countries. Badges and motivational nudges significantly increased risk-taking, especially under high volatility and for inexperienced, low-literacy traders. A **one-SD rise in financial literacy cut the effect by 56%**. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/beexfi/v46y2025ics2214635025000309.html)

**Pump-and-dump groups**
- **Hamrick, Rouhi, Mukherjee, Feder, Gandal, Moore & Vasek (2021), "An examination of the cryptocurrency pump-and-dump ecosystem," *Information Processing & Management* (July 2021).** Identified **952 pumps on Discord and 2,469 on Telegram** over six months in 2018 (working paper: about 4,818 total, 1,051 Discord + 3,767 Telegram), across about 2,000 coins on 220 exchanges. **10%** of Telegram (Discord) pumps raised the price by more than **18% (12%) within 5 minutes**. Obscure low-cap coins were the most profitable targets. — [HUJI CRIS](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/an-examination-of-the-cryptocurrency-pump-and-dump-ecosystem/); [CEPR DP13404](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP13404.pdf)
- **Li, Shin & Wang, "The Economics of Cryptocurrency Pump and Dump Schemes" (SSRN 3310307; WEIS 2019).** Economic analysis of crypto pump-and-dump schemes. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3310307); [VoxEU column](https://cepr.org/article/economics-cryptocurrency-pump-and-dump-schemes)

### Inferences
- Taken together: leaderboards ranked on raw cumulative return attract flows (Röder & Walter), do not predict copier alpha (Dorfleitner et al.), and create tournament and convex incentives that push leaders toward lottery-like bets (Oehler & Schneider; Kirchler). A product ranking leaders on risk-adjusted, drawdown-aware, longer-horizon metrics directly addresses the documented failure mode. Dorfleitner et al. found that Sharpe-based selection improved results.
- The Pelster & Hofmann result implies social visibility itself distorts leader behaviour. Showing "unrealised loss duration" or forcing stop-loss transparency could help.
- Gamification harms concentrate among low-literacy users, the same group regulators most want to protect. Hedonic rewards for trade activity (confetti, streaks) carry the most risk.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study using Bybit, Binance or Bitget copy-trading data was found. Crypto copy-trading evidence is industry or secondary only.
- No ZuluTrade-specific peer-reviewed paper was retrieved in this pass.
- Scheckenbach et al. (2021) on social trading risk-taking appeared only as a citation and is unverified.
- The ICIS 2019 and Deng et al. (eToro, *Information Systems Research*) papers were seen only in summaries.

## 4. Consumer protection gaps and recommended safeguards a product could adopt voluntarily

### Takeaway
The main gaps are: leaders who are unregulated retail clients but act like advisers or portfolio managers; suitability or appropriateness checks that are easy to game (ASIC v eToro); conflicted lead-trader pay tied to follower counts or volume; and crypto activity outside traditional investor compensation. Regulators' toolkit offers ready-made voluntary safeguards: suitability gating with blocking of unsuitable copied trades, leverage limits, audited "% of copiers losing money" disclosures, five-year fair past-performance presentation, 24-hour cooling-off, no activity incentives, lead-trader vetting and removal, and monitoring of copier outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- **Suitability and blocking.** ESMA expects IT controls so "unsuitable transactions are not automatically copied", monitoring of copied trades against client risk limits with intervention, and protection against a copied trader switching to unsuitable instruments. — [ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Leader vetting and limits.** ESMA asks whether firms evaluate people ex ante before their trades become copyable and set limits on their trading activity. IOSCO calls for procedures to select and remove lead traders. — [ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf); [CMS on IOSCO](https://cms.law/en/int/regulatory-news/iosco-online-imitative-trading-practices-copy-trading-mirror-trading-social-trading)
- **Conflicted pay.** ESMA flags variable pay based on number or value of trades copied, number of followers, or AUM, and requires inducement disclosure to copiers. — [ESMA35-42-1428, section 2.6](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf)
- **Unregulated influencers.** IOSCO identifies a gap for "unregistered individuals who influence retail investors without the professional qualifications or oversight". — [Mondo Visione/IOSCO](https://mondovisione.com/news/iosco-publishes-final-reports-on-finfluencers-online-imitative-trading-practice-2025519)
- **Gameable gating.** ASIC alleges eToro's screening test let clients retake it without limit and prompted them toward passing answers. — [ASIC 23-204MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2023-releases/23-204mr-asic-sues-etoro-in-its-first-design-and-distribution-action-to-protect-consumers-from-high-risk-cfd-products/)
- **Leverage and loss limits.** ESMA's 2018 CFD measures (30:1 to 2:1 leverage, margin close-out, negative balance protection, incentive bans, standardised loss-percentage warning) and ASIC's 2021 order (91% fall in losses) are proven safeguards. — [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933); [ASIC 22-082MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2022-releases/22-082mr-asic-s-cfd-product-intervention-order-extended-for-five-years/)
- **Cooling-off and appropriateness friction.** FCA crypto rules: 24-hour cooling-off for first-time investors, appropriateness test with a 24-hour wait before a second retake, ban on incentives to invest. The FCA's 2024 review found poor practice where "proceed" and "leave" options lacked equal prominence. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/fca-publishes-final-rules-and-supplementary-draft-guidance-on-financial-promotions-for-cryptoassets); [FCA review](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/assessing-compliance-back-end-cryptoasset-financial-promotions-rules)
- **Honest performance marketing.** ESMA: past performance must not be the most prominent element, should cover 5 years, no simulated past performance, fees disclosed. SEC: no overstated AI claims (Delphia, Global Predictions). CFTC: be sceptical of bots promising high or guaranteed returns. — [ESMA35-42-1428](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-03/ESMA35-42-1428_Supervisory_Briefing_on_Copy_Trading.pdf); [O'Melveny](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/sec-targets-ai-washing-in-charges-against-two-investment-advisers); [CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)
- **Influencer supervision.** FINRA (2023 sweep, M1 Finance $850k): separate procedures for influencer programmes, background checks and training. — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-and-legislation/news/finra-fines-m1-finance-in-landmark-social-media-enforcement-250948)
- **De-gamification.** Hedonic features raise trading volume and risk-taking, especially for low-literacy users. Financial literacy cuts the effect by 56% per SD. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/epdf/10.1287/mnsc.2022.02650); [JBEF 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/beexfi/v46y2025ics2214635025000309.html)

### Inferences
- A voluntary safeguard stack consistent with the sources above:
  1. Suitability or appropriateness questionnaire that cannot be gamed: limited retakes, a waiting period, no hints toward passing answers.
  2. Per-copier risk caps (max allocation, max leverage, drawdown stop) enforced automatically, with unsuitable leader trades blocked rather than copied.
  3. Leader eligibility vetting, trading limits and a removal process.
  4. Leader pay not tied purely to follower counts or volume, disclosed to copiers.
  5. Leaderboards ranked on risk-adjusted, multi-period metrics with five-year or full-history display and no simulated returns.
  6. Prominent, periodically updated "% of copier accounts that lost money (net of fees)".
  7. 24-hour cooling-off for first-time users.
  8. No confetti, streaks or activity rewards.
  9. No "AI" claims that cannot be substantiated.
- Liability: under ESMA's analysis, when copying is automatic the *firm* is the portfolio manager and carries suitability responsibility. This suggests platform operators, not lead traders, bear the regulatory risk for losses from unsuitable copied trades.

### Gaps
- **Investor compensation for crypto.** I did not retrieve a primary source confirming that crypto copy-trading losses fall outside FSCS, ICF (Cyprus) or SIPC cover. FCA crypto risk warnings state that investors are unlikely to be protected if something goes wrong, but the exact wording was not verified in this pass.
- **Liability allocation** between platform, lead trader and bot provider: no court ruling or regulator statement on civil liability for losses caused by a lead trader or bot was found. The ESMA classification is the closest guidance.
- **Tax reporting complexity** from many small copied trades: no regulator or academic source was found in this pass.
- **Cooling-off or limit rules specific to copy trading** (as opposed to crypto promotions or CFDs) were not found in any jurisdiction.
